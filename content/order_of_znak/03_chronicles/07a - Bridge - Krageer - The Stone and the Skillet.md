# 🪨 The Stone and the Skillet: The Saga of Krageer in the High Country

> **Chronicle Navigation:** ⬅️ **Back to:** [[07 - Epilogue - Bridge Campaign|Epilogue: Bridge Campaign]] | 🏠 [[The Order of Znak Complete Campaign Chronicle|Campaign Chronicle Hub]]

---

*What [[Krageer Stoneface Ogolakanu]] did in the years between the training of the [[The Acolytes of Znak Campaign Plan|Acolytes]] and the gathering that would become the [[07 - Epilogue - Bridge Campaign|Bridge Campaign]].*

When the [[The Order of Znak|Order of Znak]] scattered after the training of the Acolytes, each champion went where their own road led. Krageer's road led to a woman with flour on her fingers, then north, then up. He was gone nearly four years, four winters in the high country. Of those years he said only, "There was a war, and then a dragon. Both ended." This is the longer account, as the Ogolakanu tell it. They tell it loudly and with a good deal of gesturing.

*A note on names. A goliath has three of them: a short birth name, a nickname for everyday use, and a long clan name kept for ceremony and heritage. The tongue they speak is Gol-Kaa, which is mostly vowels and soft consonants, and every proper name in it ends on a vowel. Krageer's own name is the exception. His aunt says it was Kageleo before the lowlands got hold of it. How, he has never said.*

## I. The Road North

It began as a holiday. Krageer did not know what a holiday was, so [[Cooragh|Cooragh Struckt]] explained it. You went somewhere with no one to kill, you ate, and you stayed until you got bored. They took a slow road along the coast, and she cooked over every fire. By the fourth week Krageer had stopped counting how many pork pies he had eaten and begun counting how many he had left.

On a cliff above the sea, he stared at the water for most of an hour. Then he said, "We should marry."

Cooragh looked up from the skillet. "Is that a question?"

"It is a plan."

She boomed with laughter, flicked flour at his nose, and said yes. Then she asked where, because she would not be married in a ditch. Krageer thought for a long time. "My people marry on the mountain," he said. "Under open sky. With the stones. Kavaki watches."

"Who?"

"The Ram-Lord. He made us. He likes a fair fight and a good wedding. He is not particular about the order."

He had not seen his mountains in more years than he would admit. So they turned north. They passed the Silver Marches and kept climbing into the cold, where the road gave out and the wind took over. Krageer's pace quickened. His shoulders, which Cooragh had assumed were already as straight as they could go, straightened further.

*Game note: Mountain Born meant the thin air and the cold troubled him not at all. Cooragh complained of both loudly and constantly, and kept up.*

## II. Pelukanemo, the Warm Hollow (Autumn of the First Year)

The Spine of the World is a wall of ice, and goliaths wear very little. The Ogolakanu have therefore always wintered in **Pelukanemo** (the Warm Hollow). It is a valley hidden among the frozen peaks, where old volcanic vents breathe steam through the rock. There the snow melts off the grass, and a goliath can stand about in his skin and be comfortable. Cooragh and Krageer crested the last ridge in the autumn, with the first snow at their heels. She stopped complaining about the cold the moment they came over. She started complaining about the sulfur.

Pelukanemo was not warm in any other way. The first camp was empty, its cairns toppled. The second held a dozen hunters with arrows nocked. They lowered them when a scarred goliath pulled off his Dread Helm. Its crimson eyes had made them nervous. His face made them stare. "Stoneface," one whispered. "He has come back."

Chieftain **Menuka**, called **Windfist**, of the Ogolakanu, was Krageer's mother's sister. She had raised him, and she was gray now, with one arm in a sling. She did not embrace him. Goliaths do not greet that way. She looked him up and down. She took in the scale mail, the sleeve of tribal ink, the lightning bolt, and the tally of the Fallen running down his ribs.

"Kageleo," she said. "You have written all over yourself. The skywatchers will say you tried to rewrite your fate."

"I did," said Krageer. "It was a bad fate."

Beside him, Cooragh's eyebrow climbed at the strange word. She opened her mouth, looked at the chieftain's sling and the hard line of her jaw, and shut it again. *Later,* said the eyebrow.

"Hm. Who is the orc?"

"Half," said Cooragh. "And his bride, if the mountain permits."

"The mountain permits nothing," said Menuka. "Sit. We have bad news and worse news."

The bad news was war. Three neighboring clans had been raiding and counter-raiding the Ogolakanu for two winters. They were the **Mamaluko**, the **Ivonakega**, and the **Thekupala**. These were not strangers. They were the same people from the same mountains, who had always traded furs and meat to the giants and dwarves side by side and settled their grudges with games. Now herds were stolen, a skywatcher was killed, and a dawncaller was found dead at her post. Nobody could remember who had started it. Each clan was proud, and each owed another an unpaid insult, so it kept going.

The worse news was the white shape. Shepherds had seen it crossing the high passes, vast and pale and silent, with the frost-fog trailing it. Whole flocks had vanished. High pastures had gone dead white overnight. Its fog was no one's weapon against the clans. It simply drove them down off the cold ground and onto each other's grazing, and every clan crowded into the few warm valleys that remained.

"A dragon," Krageer said.

"Yes."

"White."

"Yes. Why?"

Krageer touched the Dragon Slayer at his hip. "I have killed one," he said. "He was young. This one is not."

Menuka did not waste breath. No goliath keeps a written law, and a chieftain holds her seat only by proving every day that she is fit to hold it. Menuka had one good arm and no peace to show for two winters. She needed help, and every morning after breakfast she named her Task Captains from among the clan, as the Ogolakanu have always done. The result matters more than the method. The morning after Krageer arrived, she named him Task Captain of Walking Between Fires. He understood at once that this was the hardest task of the day.

## III. The Open-Hand Parley (The First Winter)

Krageer had never been good at talking. But among goliaths, a person who says exactly what they mean is a rare and respected thing.

He and Cooragh spent the whole of the first winter on the long paths between camps, with Menuka's leave and her token. Under the custom of **Mevalinuko** (the Open-Hand Parley), a goliath who comes unarmed to a rival's fire is owed a hearing. A goliath who comes with a half-orc cook is owed a very strange one.

**The Thekupala** held out longest. Their leader, **Linaku**, called **Flint-Ear**, listened to Krageer's offer with her arms crossed. The offer was peace, shared grazing, and a council before the dragon took everything. When he finished she said, "That was not a speech."

"No," said Krageer. "It was a fact."

*[Persuasion 1d20 - 2 (CHA 6, not proficient) = 17 (natural 19) vs. DC 17. Success. Two cooks at a nearby fire made a note never to speak of this.]*

Cooragh took the other half of the diplomacy. She pitched her kitchen in the middle of the Thekupala camp and made pork pies, one batch plain and one in her fiercest style. Within an hour grown goliaths who had sneered at a half-orc bride were drinking snowmelt by the gallon, eyes streaming. Nobody wished to be the first to admit defeat.

*[Cook's utensils: 1d20 (16) + 5 = 21 vs. DC 15. A fine pie. The Thekupala elders agreed that anyone who could do that to them could stay.]*

**The Ivonakega** were harder. They were the Ogolakanu's oldest rivals, and their leader, **Kenuvi**, called **Slow-Fox**, was cunning. He would not talk until Krageer talked, and Krageer would not talk until there was something to say. So Kenuvi passed him the flagon, which is how Drink-and-Tell begins. Each drinker takes a swallow of mead and trades a tale. Kenuvi told three, each longer than the last. Krageer drank. At last Kenuvi raised an eyebrow, and Krageer said, "There was a dragon. I killed it."

That was the whole tale. Kenuvi waited for the rest. Krageer sat three more days without adding to it, and Kenuvi watched him the entire time, looking for the lie.

*[House-ruled as a contest between Krageer's sincerity and Kenuvi's suspicion. Krageer: Persuasion 1d20 (17) - 2 = 15. Kenuvi: Insight 1d20 (7) + 4 = 11. Krageer wins. A stone could not be made to hide anything, and Kenuvi could not find the crack.]*

On the fourth day Kenuvi surrendered his grievance and asked what Krageer wanted. "Your hunters at my aunt's side when the dragon comes," Krageer said. "That is all."

**The Mamaluko** would not listen. Their leader, **Pagolu**, called **the Ram-Breaker**, was a mountain of a goliath. He had earned the name by shoving a rutting ram off a ridge in a game of King of the Hill, and no one had let him forget it. The elders said Kavaki had found it funny. Pagolu cared for nothing but strength. If the Ogolakanu wished to speak for the clans, Krageer would have to prove he could carry them.

The challenge was set on the old flat stone above the Mamaluko camp, in the last cold weeks of the winter. All three clans came to watch, and bets were made in goats. By custom the contest would be arm against arm, best of three: no tricks, no tools, no weapons. Rage is not a trick; it is a goliath. A woman beside the stone struck a gong. Krageer put his arm on the rock.

*House-rule contest: each round is a contested Strength (Athletics) check. Krageer is +10 (STR +5, proficiency +5). Pagolu is +9.*

- **Round 1.** Krageer rolled 14 + 10 = **24**. Pagolu rolled 10 + 9 = **19**. Krageer won, and the hill roared.
- **Round 2.** Pagolu rolled a natural 20 for **29**. Krageer rolled 12 + 10 = **22**. Pagolu won. His arm came down like a falling tree and slammed Krageer's knuckles into the rock hard enough to chip it.
- **Round 3.** Krageer rolled 15 + 10 = **25**. Pagolu rolled 16 + 9 = **25**. They tied, and the hill went quiet. Krageer stood, rolled his neck, and let the Rage take him. His eyes went flat. Rage grants advantage on Strength checks. For the tie-break he rolled **8 and 17**, keeping 17 for **27**. Pagolu rolled 14 + 9 = **23**. The Ram-Breaker's arm folded slowly onto the stone.

Pagolu looked at his hand for a long while. Then he laughed, loud and cracked and delighted, and clasped Krageer's forearm. "You are the first to ever make me *work*."

Krageer nodded. "You are second."

Pagolu signed his clan to the pact before the next sunrise. Menuka, hearing of it, named the united whole **Mevolukanipa** (the Four Fires). Nobody would call it a tribe, because tribes are for people who have stopped arguing.

## IV. The War of Milagove (The Second Spring)

Peace did not come all at once. Two Ivonakega warbands refused the pact outright, and in the second spring they raided a Mamaluko herd. Kenuvi spoke of shunning them (*volok-thea*), but the old cure only turned grievance into spite. The old war flared in a single day, and the new alliance cracked down the middle.

It broke at **Milagove** (Cinder Ford), a shallow crossing at the bottom of a black volcanic gorge, where the water ran warm and smelled of struck flint. Eighty raiders held the far bank. The united clans numbered a hundred and sixty, but green and quarrelsome, with a long line of pride between each warband. Menuka named Krageer Task Captain of the Line. It was the first time she had ever named anyone war-captain, and she did it with a face like a shut door.

Krageer did what he knew how to do, and he did it from the front. He took the center of the ford, planted [[Ultima Exodus]] in the gravel, and sang. It was an old Ogolakanu war chant with no real tune, just a drone of hard syllables, but it carried down the whole line. Goliaths from three clans who had never agreed on anything began beating time on their shields. The line stopped cracking and began to move together.

He held the ford for most of a day. At one point he stood in the water against nine raiders, with Cooragh on his left, Extra Attack in her stride and a plain iron greatsword in her hands. She cut down four of them herself. *(Later, back at the [[The Wayside Inn|Wayside Inn]], Krageer would hum that chant over the hearth one evening. A young bard named [[Smithwell Znak]] would say it was the first song that ever made him want to fight.)*

When the raiders broke, Krageer did not pursue. He walked across the ford and offered the survivors water. The war was over by the next full moon.

Those who fought alongside him said he had learned something that day, not about fighting but about *leading*. Men and women he had never met moved when he moved, and they were counting on him not to fall. He had always fought for the people at his side. Now he saw how many people there were.

*That season Krageer reached **Fighter 5**. It was hard-won command experience, earned in a long war and paid for in bruises.*

Menuka offered him the chieftainship. In goliath custom that means someone else must be shoved off it, but she was prepared to do the shoving herself. He thought about it for the length of a single breath. "I am the Order's," he said. "I would leave you one day." She nodded as if she had expected nothing else. She named Pagolu, who had never been outdone by anyone but Krageer, as war-leader. Linaku became the voice of the council. The Four Fires had a shape at last. They needed only the dragon to give them a reason to keep it.

## V. The Pale Widow's Trail (The Third Year and the Fourth Spring)

The third year was the slow, unglamorous work, and Krageer was worse at it than at war. The council met at each fire in turn, so that no clan could say it had been made to climb to another's. The clans began to trade with the giants and dwarves as one voice instead of four, and got better prices for it. Grazing rotated across the warm valleys by a schedule that Linaku kept in her head and defended like a fortress. Nobody drew a blade on a neighbor. Krageer called this the hardest fight of his life, and Pagolu said that was only because it had no end.

Cooragh kept the kitchen of the Four Fires, and kept the count of the seasons. The Binding of Stones needs a peaceful mountain and witnesses from all four fires, and for two and a half years the mountain had been neither. "I have been engaged," she told a Mamaluko shepherd, "longer than some of your marriages have *lasted*." She could have gone south to wait at the Wayside Inn, and Krageer offered. She would not. She would not leave him to fight alone, and by now the Thekupala followed her ladle nearly as readily as they followed his axe. She had been in the line at Milagove. She was not waiting for a war to end. She was in it.

All the while the frost-fog spread. It crept outward from a single ice-choked valley in the northern heights, a little further each year, and within it nothing lived and nothing moved. The clans took to calling the beast the Pale Widow, because the old shepherds said she keened at night. By the third winter's end, when the thaw revealed how far the white had come, a Mamaluko fur-trader named **Gulemo**, called **Longpack**, came down from the frost giants' halls with news he had not wanted.

A white dragon had been asking the giants about a goliath of the Ogolakanu. The giants knew the tale. The slaying of [[Cryovain]] at [[Icespire Hold]] had traveled far, up to [[Dagult Neverember]] in Neverwinter and the Lords of Waterdeep. The roads had carried it north in the mouths of merchants, and the giants had it from them. They knew of a famous Goliath Barbarian in a crimson-eyed helm with a dragon-killing sword. Any giant who had ever haggled with the Ogolakanu knew the pattern of the tribal sleeve on his right arm, and knew which hollows the clan wintered in, for the Ogolakanu had traded with the giants at the Warm Hollow's gates for generations.

A single dragon cannot be fought by a hundred and sixty goliaths without losing most of them. Krageer said so at council, and the clans grumbled. "The dragon will not come for you," he told them. "It will come for *me*."

Nobody doubted him. They had all seen the long tally of the Fallen on his left ribs, and the lightning bolt in it. Whoever carried that much death was going to meet something that wanted to settle accounts.

So the fourth spring was spent in preparation. The Ivonakega trackers found the trail. Linaku's scouts mapped the approaches. Pagolu drilled forty archers to cover the retreat. On the eve of the march, Krageer announced that he would go alone to the valley's mouth.

Cooragh set down her ladle.

"No."

"This is not your fight," he said.

"I will tell you which fights are mine." She took his stern face in both hands and held it as if it were bread dough. "I came north with you to be married. I have waited two and a half years for it. I will not be a widow before the cake."

Nobody remembers what Krageer said next. The Ogolakanu have six versions and all of them end the same way. She went with him.

## VI. Kuvimeno, the Glass Hall (Late in the Fourth Spring)

The valley was a long throat of blue ice under a sky bruised with cloud, and at its end a cavern opened like a mouth. The clans halted at the mouth of the valley as ordered. Two walked on.

Krageer wore the Dread Helm, and Ultima Exodus rode on his back. In his hand was the Dragon Slayer. It was a longsword, claimed from [[Dragon Barrow]] and blooded on Cryovain at Icespire Hold.

The cavern was a vaulted hall of translucent ice, which the old shepherds called **Kuvimeno** (the Glass Hall). The Pale Widow waited in it. She was an **Adult White Dragon** (*Monster Manual*), vast as a house and the color of a bone in a snowfield. She had a long scar down her flank, very old. She raised her head and spoke in a voice like a glacier tearing.

"You are the one who wears the dead one's death. The Goliath. The Barbarian."

"I am Krageer."

"I am **Hrimgarra**." The great head swung low. "The humans sing your names in a city by the sea, and the singing climbed the roads to the giants. The giants spoke of a red-eyed helm and a sword that ends my kind, and of a mark on your arm that told me where to look. I was the elder of my clutch. Cryovain was the youngest."

The cavern went very still. Krageer said nothing, as he always did, and let her speak.

She told it in pieces. When the frost giants of Jarl Storvald took the youngest of her clutch, she had been hunting three valleys away. She had seen the longships come. She had been afraid. She had *fled* before the chains went on. She flew a year south and a year more, and every night she heard a younger dragon screaming across a thousand miles of sky. When Cryovain escaped and made his lair at Icespire Hold, Hrimgarra had been too ashamed to answer his call. Then she heard that Cryovain was dead.

"I was not there when it was chained," said the dragon. "I was not there when it fell. I have come to find the one who ended it." The fog around her went cold. "If I take your life, I answer the death. If you take mine, I pay for the abandoning. Either way my shame is *finished*."

Krageer considered this for some time.

"He was young," he said. "He was cruel. He was in pain. He killed many. We killed him quickly, and I do not regret it. But I will not tell you he was not your kin." He drew a breath. "I did not kill him alone."

"I did not come for the others."

"Then I accept your duel. Kavaki favors a fair fight, and I will give you one."

Hrimgarra bowed her head, and the strangest thing happened. A long, bone-deep exhale ran through the ice like a sigh. "Then your ram and I agree on one thing. On the ice, on the ground, one to one. I will not take the sky. Let no one say I was not *fair*."

"One to one," said Krageer. And Cooragh Struckt stepped out of the dark beside him with an iron greatsword in her hands.

"That is not one to one," said the dragon.

"No," said Cooragh. "It's two for one. Pick your poison."

### The Duel

*[Cooragh, Wis save: 1d20 (14) + 1 = 15. Success. "I've seen worse in a kitchen," she said, which was not true, but she said it with great conviction.]*

The dragon's bite found Krageer first, before the Rage had taken him.

At the end of his turn Hrimgarra spent two of her **legendary actions** on a **Wing Attack**. Both heroes were within ten feet. Her wings came down like a falling door.

Then the dragon beat her wings and rose forty feet into the hall. She had given her word, and she broke it.

**Round 2. Cooragh.** She got her knees under her. She stood, spat, and clamped a hand over her ribs. A Fighter learns to find a second wind when she needs one.

*[She stood (15 ft of movement) and used Second Wind: 1d10 (4) + 5 = 9. Cooragh: 12 HP.]*

**Round 3. Hrimgarra.** The dragon dove. She stooped on Cooragh like a hawk on a hare.

*[Frightful Presence again: both had already shrugged it off, and it did nothing. Bite on Cooragh: 1d20 (15) + 11 = 26 vs. AC 17. Hit. 2d10 (8, 5) + 6 = 19 piercing, plus 1d8 (4) cold. Total 23. Cooragh dropped to 0.]*

But Cooragh Struckt was half orc, and a half-orc does not stay down on the first count. **Relentless Endurance.** She dropped to 0, and she rose at **1 HP**, spitting blood. She grabbed her sword and said something about Krageer's mother that a lady does not repeat.

*[The dragon's claws went for Krageer. He had attacked recklessly last round, so she had advantage. First claw: 1d20 (12, 18) + 11 = 29. Hit. 2d6 (5, 6) + 6 = 17. Stone's Endurance: 1d12 (6) + 3 = 9, so 17 - 9 = 8, and the Bear halved it to **4**. The tattoo of the Fallen on his left ribs burned hot under the blood. Second claw: 1d20 (2, 5) + 11 = 16. Miss.]*

Krageer had seen her fall.

He did not roar. That was the terrible part. He went very quiet, and the Rage that had burned in him turned to something colder and steadier. For the first time in his life he was fighting not for honor, not for the Order, and not for the dead, but for one living person.

**Round 3. Krageer.** Krageer looked at Cooragh, swaying on the ice at one hit point. Then he looked up. The dragon hung ten feet above the ice, still over her, jaws open. Krageer did not think about the rules. He had one chance and used it.

**Action Surge.** With the first action he *ran* and *jumped*. It was a great stone-footed leap off the broken ice, higher than any man should reach. He landed astride the dragon's neck, and his left arm locked under her jaw. The Dragon Slayer stayed in his right fist. With the second action he made the Attack action. He gave one of the two attacks to something no goliath had a name for.

**House-ruled feat of strength: the haul-down.** *[House ruling for the DM to accept. Normally impossible: a Medium creature cannot grapple a Huge one, so this is not a formal grapple. The DM allows one contested Strength (Athletics) check, in place of one of Krageer's two attacks. Krageer makes it at **disadvantage** because of the size difference: roll two d20s and take the lower.]*

*[Krageer, Athletics at disadvantage: 1d20 (13, 9) take the lower, 9, + 10 = 19. Hrimgarra, Athletics: 1d20 (12) + 6 = 18. **Krageer wins by 1.** He is barely ahead.]*

He roared, and the Rage took him past anything it had taken him through before. Bear-strong, with the mountain in his back and Cooragh on the ice below, he put his whole weight against the sky and *pulled*. The dragon's wings beat once, twice, and lost. She came down like a felled tree. Her flight was cut short, and she could not take the air again until she tore free.

*[They fell ten feet together. Hrimgarra took 1d6 (3) bludgeoning. Dragon: 136 - 3 = **133**. Krageer took 1d6 (4), halved by Rage resistance to **2**. Until she escapes, Hrimgarra cannot fly. She must spend her action to break free.]*

With his sword hand he struck, from her back, with his arm still locked under her jaw.

*[Dragon Slayer, Reckless Attack (advantage): 1d20 (14, 16) + 11 = 27, hit. Damage: 1d8 (5) + 9 + 3d6 vs. dragon (3, 4, 3 = 10) = 5 + 9 + 10 = 24. Dragon: 133 - 24 = **109**.]*

**Round 3. Cooragh.** Cooragh was up. She was bloody and swaying, and she did not hesitate. She lifted her greatsword in both hands and brought it down on the base of the dragon's wing.

*[Greatsword: 1d20 = 20. Natural 20. Critical. Savage Attacks: 5d6 (3, 6, 4, 5, 6) = 24, + 3 = 27. Dragon: 109 - 27 = **82**. Second attack: 1d20 (12) + 6 = 18. Hit. 2d6 (3, 4) = 7, + 3 = 10. Dragon: 82 - 10 = **72**.]*

*(The Ogolakanu, who retell this part with their whole arms, always stop here to point out one thing. At that moment the dragon had legendary actions left, and she spent none. The clans have argued over why for years. Krageer, riding her neck, had no time to wonder.)*

**Round 4. Hrimgarra.** The dragon thrashed. The ice shook. She wrenched against Krageer's arm with all her weight. It took her entire action.

*[Same house ruling: Krageer holds at disadvantage because of the size difference. Dragon escapes, Athletics: 1d20 (16) + 6 = 22. Krageer, contested Athletics at disadvantage: 1d20 (16, 14) take the lower, 14, + 10 = 24. **Held, by 2.** She stays grounded.]*

**Round 4. Krageer.** Then he struck. It was a swing from the heels, and the Dragon Slayer sang down in his hands.

*[Dragon Slayer, Reckless Attack: 1d20 (19, 6) + 11 = 30. Natural 19 is a crit under Improved Critical. Dice are doubled, with Brutal Critical adding one weapon die: 3d8 (6, 4, 7) = 17. Plus 6d6 vs. dragon (3, 5, 2, 6, 4, 1) = 21, plus 9. Total 17 + 21 + 9 = 47. Dragon: 72 - 47 = **25**. His second swing: 1d20 (3, 6) + 11 = 17. Miss.]*

**Round 4. Cooragh.** Cooragh struck, with all the weight of her wedding promise behind her.

*[Greatsword: 1d20 (14) + 6 = 20. Hit. 2d6 (5, 4) = 9, + 3 = 12. Dragon: 25 - 12 = **13**. Second attack: 1d20 (3) + 6 = 9. Miss.]*

**Round 5. Hrimgarra.** The dragon stopped.

## VII. The Pale Widow's Last Gift

She lay on the ice, with her wings half-folded and her blue eyes dull. She did not rise and did not try. Krageer still sat astride her neck, his left arm locked under her jaw and the Dragon Slayer low in his right hand. He did not strike, and for a moment no one spoke.

"I broke my word," said Hrimgarra. "I took the sky."

"You did."

"I am tired of breaking my word." Her breath rattled. "You killed my youngest. I came to answer it. I have killed shepherds in your mountains, and my fog drove your clans onto each other's grazing until they bled. That is not an answer. That is more shame."

Krageer looked at her, and then, to the surprise of everyone who knew him, he said something that was not blunt. "You came north to find its death, and you found its killers. You came to face us and did not run." He unlocked his arm, slid down her shoulder to the ice, and stood. "That is more than you did at the chains."

Hrimgarra shut her eyes. "Then let me have an end that is not a fleeing." She lifted her head one last time. "Ask nothing of the hoard. Take it. Make it *right.*"

"Do you ask this of me?" said Krageer.

"I ask."

He drew the Dragon Slayer, and he struck once, clean, and the dragon's long breath went out into the ice. She had not been avenged and she had not been cursed. She had been given what she came for, an honorable death at an honest hand, and a last act of atonement.

*[The duel ended with Hrimgarra at 13 hit points, and with a choice. Krageer chose.]*

Cooragh sat down very suddenly on the ice and put her head between her knees. Krageer sat beside her. After a time he said, "You are bleeding."

"I know."

"You were very good."

"I know that too." She leaned her head against his huge shoulder. "Stop talking. I am proud of you."

For a while he did not talk. He looked at the long white shape on the ice. "She took the sky once," he said at last. "After I brought her down, she did not take it again. She had the tricks, and she used none of them." He was quiet. "She kept her word the second time."

Cooragh patted his knee, which for her was a speech.

*That season, in the fourth spring, Krageer reached **Fighter 6**. No dragon is slain without teaching the slayer something, and he had learned it in the Glass Hall.*

### The Hoard

Hrimgarra's hoard lay in the deep chambers: coin, gems, and old trade goods stripped from caravans the dragon had burned. Krageer took none of it. Neither did Cooragh.

The clan chiefs came to the cavern, and Krageer said what the dragon had said. The hoard went to a **Pamolivu** (Peace-Hoard), held in common and sealed under the council's stones. With no written law to bind them, the four clans swore to it aloud, before witnesses, as goliaths always have. The hoard paid the blood-price for every herd and every life the war and the dragon had cost. It rebuilt the toppled cairns, bought winter grain, and ransomed prisoners. The Ogolakanu, Mamaluko, Ivonakega, and Thekupala each kept an equal key. The oldest skywatchers carried a handful of coins to the dragon's body and set them in the ice around her, because a debt had been paid and a debt deserves a witness. A lamentor sang over her, which no one had ever done for a dragon before.

## VIII. Kolipemu, the Binding of Stones (The Fourth Autumn)

With the dragon dead, the fog drew back, and for the first time in four years the mountain was at peace with all four fires on it. Cooragh did not intend to wait one more season. The wedding was held at the first full moon of that autumn, on the high shoulder of the mountain where Menuka had been wed and her mother before her. It followed the ancient Ogolakanu rite of **Kolipemu** (the Binding of Stones), under the eye of Kavaki the Ram-Lord. The clerics say he made the first goliaths from gems that grew on a bush he kept on the highest peak, and every stone carried up the mountain goes home to its source.

Each partner carries a river stone up the mountain alone and sets it at the cairn. The stones must hold each other, so they are set in a single balance. The Ogolakanu tent-mother, **Ekulu**, called **Warm-Lap**, examined the bride, as is her office, to judge whether she was old enough for daily tasks. Ekulu pronounced her ready. "She has been ready since the day she was born," said Ekulu, who was not wrong.

The bride is also asked three questions by the elders. *What do you bring? What do you earn? What will you carry?* In the old days a bride's answer could take an hour. Cooragh's took one breath.

"A skillet," she said. "My name. Anything he can't."

The elders laughed until two of them had to sit down. Menuka, who never laughed at anything, wiped her eyes. She named Cooragh **Thaluu**, a Gol-Kaa word meaning "she who holds the fire." A goliath has three names, so Cooragh was now a goliath in all three: Cooragh for birth, Thaluu for the fire, and Ogolakanu by marriage. The Four Fires cheered themselves hoarse.

Then the elders turned to the groom, as the rite requires, and Menuka gave his three names to the mountain. "Kageleo, called Stoneface, of the Ogolakanu."

Cooragh leaned over. She did not lean quietly. "That's twice now. Who is Kageleo?"

"Me."

"Since when?"

"Birth."

"Then who," said Cooragh, "have I been sleeping next to?"

Krageer considered the question with the gravity he gave to all questions. "I joined a lowland company young. The sergeant at the muster table could not say my name. He could not spell it either. He heard an *r* in it somewhere and wrote down what he heard." He paused. "Krageer."

"And you told him he'd got it wrong."

"No."

"Why not?"

"The line was long." Another pause. "After that it was on every roll, every pay chit, every contract. A name that travels is useful. So I let Kageleo stay in the mountains."

Cooragh stared at him. Several elders were pretending not to listen, and doing it badly. "You let a man misspell you for *years*."

"Yes."

She reached up and booped his nose, hard enough to count as an argument. "Fine. Krageer in the kitchen. Kageleo when you're in trouble."

"I am rarely in trouble."

"You're marrying me."

Krageer opened his mouth, closed it, and nodded once. Across the cairn, Menuka said, "Hm. She learns quickly." It was the nearest thing to a blessing the chieftain had.

Cooragh looked at the cairn. "Is there a bush for half-orcs?"

"No," said Ekulu. "You will have to grow your own."

The feast went three days. The Ogolakanu had never fed a Thekupala or an Ivonakega at one fire. Cooragh cooked for all of them. Three days of her fiercest pork pies were served to four hundred people. Between courses the clans played goat-ball on the high ledges, because competition decides who contributed most by evening, and the wedding is no exception. Two earth genasi traders came up the pass and lost spectacularly at King of the Hill, as the ancient friendly rivalry requires. On the last night Pagolu wept openly into his plate and swore a blood-oath never to fight her.

### The Mark

On the last night, Krageer sat before a fire while the only tattooist in the Four Fires worked on his right shoulder. She was a Thekupala woman whom everyone was a little careful around, because to draw on the skin is to rewrite one's fate. She had been told twice that she could not do this. Krageer had made her promise it would be the *last* new mark for a while. He had not meant it.

"You already wrote on yourself," she said. "More than any three of us."

"Yes."

"Why more?"

He looked across the fire at Cooragh, who was arguing with Ekulu about the correct amount of lard.

"Because that fate was bad," he said. "This one I chose."

The new tattoo sat just below the old tribal sleeve: a white dragon's eye ringed in a circle of river stones, with a small pie crust curled at its edge. Cooragh had insisted on the pie. She had also, once she heard what the tattooist was for, insisted on checking the spelling. There was none to check. It was a picture. She looked a little disappointed.

The left-hand tally of the Fallen stayed as it was. He did not add an X for Hrimgarra, and he did not explain why.

## IX. The Pull South (The Fourth Winter)

They stayed the fourth winter, because the mountain needed it. By the end the council had no further need of a Stoneface. They had Linaku, and Pagolu, and their own long habit of quarrelling.

One evening on the ridge above the camp, Cooragh was kneading dough on a flat stone, and Krageer was sharpening Ultima Exodus. The Sending Stone at his belt, a shared item of the Order that he had carried for years and used rarely, grew faintly warm. Then it spoke, in a small clear voice that belonged to a young woman and carried the whole way from the lowlands:

"It is [[Burnice Znak|Burnice]]. We are gathering at the Wayside Inn. Please come, Krageer. We have missed you. It is time."

The stone went cold. Cooragh looked at it, and then at him. "Burnice," she said. "The paladin one? The one with the patron?"

"Yes. She was new to her training when I left her." He turned the axe over. "The young ones are calling. The others will come, when their roads end."

"Something is gathering."

"Yes."

He held the stone a long while. Then he said into it, "Coming." It was one word, and the stone, which was fussy about such things, carried it south.

"The Wayside Inn has a great many empty rooms," said Cooragh.

"Yes."

"I would like to see [[Martisha Vinetalker|Martisha]]." She flicked a pinch of flour at his nose. "And I would like to see you eat somebody else's cooking, just once, so you know how lucky you are."

Krageer considered this. It was a long consideration, even for him. "The pork pies," he said, "are still the greatest food in the realm."

"I know."

"I am not leaving because of the food."

"I *know*, you great stone." She booped his nose, and left a mark. "Pack your axe."

They went south at the first thaw of the fifth spring, a Goliath of the Ogolakanu and the woman who held the fire. Behind them the Pamolivu lay sealed under four stones, and the clans had learned to share a mountain. Ahead were old friends, and a gathering that none of them yet understood.
