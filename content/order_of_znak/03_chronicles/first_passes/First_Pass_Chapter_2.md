# 🏔️ First Pass — Chapter 2: The Winter Road & The Grandfather Tree

## Chapter 2: The Winter Road & The Grandfather Tree

*Table Session: October 28, 2026*  
*In-Game Setting: Late Autumn, 1500 DR to Deep Winter, 1501 DR (The Year of the Sea's Secrets Revealed & The Year of the Unstrung Harp)*  
*Location: Wayside Inn, Phandalin, Triboar, The Evermoor Way, The Surbrin Confluence, Yartar, & The Sacred Clearing of the Grandfather Tree (High Forest)*

The road eastward from the Sword Coast stretched across four hundred miles of changing earth. 

Tvyordiy Znak traveled at a measured, unhurried pace. While the elemental pull in his marrow was absolute, he had contemplated this departure for five years within the peaceful walls of [[Aufox Manor]]. True discipline was not measured in frantic haste, but in the unyielding cadence of each deliberate step. 

Leaving the salt air of [[Leilon]] behind, he turned north along the High Road. 

---

### 🍻 The Wayside Inn & The Hearth of Phandalin (Late Autumn, 1500 DR)

His first waypoint was the **Wayside Inn**, standing stalwart at the crossroads where the High Road met the Triboar Trail. Entering the common room beneath the modest guise of his friar's cowl, he was greeted by the rich scents of roasting mutton and spiced ale. Innkeeper Martisha and the road travelers bustled about the tables in safety—a far cry from the days when undead cultists and storm wraiths besieged the taproom. Taking a quiet corner, Tvyordiy shared a humble bowl of stew, remembering Cooragh’s boisterous laughter and the feasts of the Order. The realm was at peace, its roads secure because of the blood they had spilled.

Turning east onto the Triboar Trail along the northern foothills of the Sword Mountains, the mountain scrub turned to russet and gold. Before embarking on the lonely eastern highways, Tvyordiy turned his steps down the familiar cart-road toward **[[Phandalin]]**. 

A decade had passed since the young companions of the Order had first arrived in the frontier mining settlement with mud on their boots and copper in their pockets. Standing on the southern ridge, Tvyordiy lowered the coarse friar's cowl of his *Hat of Disguise* and smiled. The burned timber ruins and dilapidated cottages of the old frontier were long gone. In their place stood a robust, prosperous community of dressed granite, sturdy split-rail fences, and neatly stacked cordwood. The chimneys of the smelters coughed gray plumes into the crisp afternoon sky, and the distant ringing of pickaxes echoed from the hills.

He walked down the main lane without guise. In Phandalin, an Aarakocra monk was no curious stranger—he was living history.

Pushing through the heavy oak door of the **[[Stonehill Inn]]**, the blast of warmth from the central stone hearth greeted him, smelling richly of roasted turnips, braised boar ribs, and spiced blackberry cider. 

Behind the heavy counter, innkeeper [[Toblen Stonehill]] was polishing an earthenware tankard. He looked up, his cloth freezing mid-wipe. His ruddy face broke into an astonished, ear-to-ear grin.

"By the gods... Trilena! Pip! Drop those buckets!" Toblen roared into the kitchen, slamming the tankard down. *"Master Tvyordiy has come back!"*

The taproom fell dead silent for two heartbeats before erupting into a clamor of scraping bench-legs and excited shouts. Miners with rock-dust ground into their knuckles, weavers from the hillside farms, and carters in heavy sheepskin coats surged toward the hearth. 

"Ten winters, Master Monk!" bellowed an old prospector named Gurn, his white whiskers bristling as he hoisted a brimming mug of dark stout. "I still tell my grand-lads about the day on [[Icespire Hold]]! When that white devil **[[Cryovain]]** came screaming out of the blizzard, freezing our pack-mules where they stood! We were shivering in our hides, waiting for the frost-breath to turn our marrow to slush—and there you came! Bounded straight across the dragon's snout, talons flashing, and landed a single, thunderous palm right between its horned eyes!"

Gurn slammed his palm against the rough oak table with a resounding *THWACK*.

"Rattled the beast's brains right down into its tail! Stiff as a dried salt-cod in mid-air! Smashed down onto the mountain courtyard like a sack of river rocks! That was the blow that broke the winter, lads! Drink to the Order of Znak!"

The common room roared in approval, raising cups high. 

Tvyordiy accepted a bowl of piping hot potato leek soup and a cup of roasted dandelion tea, sitting cross-legged upon a low bench near the hearthstones. He listened with quiet eyes, his gravelly baritone rumbling with easy warmth:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — At the Stonehill Hearth
> *"A well-traveled road is made not by the feet of one giant, but by the steady walking of many common folk. The dragon had great wings and terrible teeth, yet here you sit, tending your hearths and feeding your children. That is the true victory over monsters."*

When Gurn and the miners pressed pouches of raw silver nuggets and frontier coin into his hands, Tvyordiy gently pushed them back across the wood. "Give it to the masonry guild for the town wall. An unbending stone is worth more than silver when the north wind blows."

Before leaving, he paid a quiet visit to **Barthen's Provisions**. [[Elmar Barthen]], now stooped with age but with the same shrewd, honest gleam in his eye, gripped Tvyordiy’s taloned forearm with both hands.

"Our trade wagons ride the road to Leilon in peace because of you and Myagkiy, brother," Barthen rasped softly. "Take this on the high trails." 

The merchant loaded his pack with smoked venison strips, hard travel biscuits, a wheel of sharp cheddar, and a tin of pure beeswax to buff his quarterstaff. With a deep bow and a protective sweep of his wings enfolding the old shopkeeper, Tvyordiy turned eastward onto the Triboar Trail toward Triboar.

### 🐎 A Week in Triboar: Apple Brandy & The Creeping Mist

East of Phandalin, the rolling foothills flattened into the vast, open plains of the Dessarin Valley. At the meeting of the Long Road and the Triboar Trail lay **Triboar**, sprawling across the prairie like a resting beast of burden.

Here, Tvyordiy drew the coarse friar's cowl of his *Hat of Disguise* low over his brow, choosing to linger for a full week. While his elemental destination called to his marrow, the discipline of the Hard Sign demanded that a traveler understand the earth he walked upon before attempting to master the sky. 

Triboar was a town of horses, riverboats, and autumn harvests. In the lively common room of the **Northshield House**, Tvyordiy took a quiet corner table beneath the cedar beams. He savored the hearty simple fare of the frontier: sweet, pan-fried river trout pulled fresh from the emerald waters of the Dessarin, warm rye loaves, and cups of crisp, in-season apple brandy freshly pressed from local valley orchards. 

Being barely fifty leagues down the cart-road from Phandalin and Leilon, the exploits of the **Order of Znak** were legendary here. From behind his modest friar's illusion, Tvyordiy listened with quiet, gleaming eyes as carters, teamsters, and wandering merchants praised the heroes of Aufox Manor over foaming tankards. They toasted the giants and monks who had broken the dragon Cryovain and shattered the storm-cults, allowing trade to flow freely from the coast to the valley. There was a profound, serene satisfaction in hearing one's life's work celebrated by common folk who had no idea the towering raptor hero sat right beside them, quietly sipping cider.

At the market stalls of the Lion's Share, Tvyordiy stocked his traveling pack with cold-weather provisions. When he purchased three large, cloth-wrapped blocks of pure rendered beef and mutton suet, the provisioner wiped his hands on his apron and raised an incredulous eyebrow:

"Three whole slabs of suet, Brother?" the merchant chuckled good-naturedly. "You must really have a soft heart for the winter songbirds! They'll sing your praises all the way to Waterdeep."

Tvyordiy’s beak curved beneath his illusion, offering a serene, pious nod: "All creatures of the sky require fat against the freezing north, good sir." In truth, the dense, high-calorie suet was essential fuel for an Aarakocra's searing avian metabolism when ascending the freezing upper troposphere.

Yet beneath the town’s autumn cheer ran an unsettling undercurrent. 

In the late evenings, as drovers gathered close around the tavern fire-pits, the talk turned hushed and uneasy. Caravan scouts down from the Evermoor fringes and the lonely eastern crossroads whispered of unnatural phenomena on the high roads. Travelers spoke of bizarre, bone-chilling white fogs that did not burn away with the morning sun—dense mists that rolled unnaturally against the prevailing wind, smelling of damp earth and old graves. A family of Vistani tinkers had passed through with dark warnings, and two separate supply wagons heading toward the eastern passes had vanished entirely inside the fog, leaving behind not a wheel-rut, a dead mule, or a solitary footprint in the dust. 

Tvyordiy made mental note of the warnings, feeling the cold weight of the signs upon his chest. 

At the end of the tenday, refreshed in body and grounded in the quiet pulse of the land, Tvyordiy packed his kit, settled his modest tab with a silver piece, and set out past the eastern stockade onto the desolate, rain-slick mud of the Evermoor Way.

### ⚔️ The Evermoor Way Ambush & The Hard Sign's Proclamation

Past Triboar, the civilized world dissolved into the vast, bleak expanse of the Evermoor Way. Here, cold autumn rains gave way to the first spitting flurries of sleet, turning the wagon tracks into deep, freezing mires. 

Two leagues west of the Surbrin crossing, black marsh smoke rose above the stunted willow boughs.

A river trading caravan had been shattered across the mud. Three hulking brutes—two slimy, river-dwelling River Trolls and a nine-foot, lichen-crusted Moss Troll—had overturned the baggage wain, cornering halfling merchant Kaelen Greenbottle and two wounded guards against the sideboard.

Breaking from the gray mist, skimming inches above the reed-tops at seventy feet of flight speed, Tvyordiy dropped his friar's disguise:
* Channeling his *Circlet of Blasting*, three incandescent beams of *Scorching Ray* streaked through the freezing drizzle, igniting the trolls' mossy hide and disabling their unnatural regeneration.
* Whirling his *Vicious Quarterstaff +2*, he struck the lead river troll with a concussive blow that locked its nerves in a brutal, paralyzed *Stunning Strike*.
* Executing the fluid, swaying redirection of the Drunken Master, he disengaged without a scratch, vaulted forty feet across the road ruts, and battered the massive Moss Troll into the splintered wagon boards with his bare fists.

When the Moss Troll raked its jagged, filth-encrusted talons across his chest, the nonmagical claws scraped harmlessly off his unyielding wereboar hide without breaking the skin. Tvyordiy did not flinch:

> *"Light some torches and attack from the rear. Fire is their foe."*

Rallying behind his iron calm, the guards thrust burning pitch torches into the beast, while Tvyordiy brought his quarterstaff down in a concussive *Crusher* critical strike that pinned the moss troll to the timbers until the guards reduced it to ash. 

Drawing his slender wand from Thunder Cliffs, Tvyordiy unleashed an eight-dart, 6th-level volley from his *Wand of Magic Missile*, obliterating the charging river troll's momentum in a staccato of force explosions before dropping into a rooted *Patient Defense*. Reeling on shattered limbs at eleven hit points, the surviving river trolls cowered in the ditch.

Tvyordiy planted the butt of his staff in the gravel, his subterranean baritone cutting through the smoke:

> **"I am Tvyordiy Znak of the Order of Znak. That is the order of Znak, not the chaos of Znak."**

Invoking *Tongue of the Sun and Moon*, his voice rumbled in ancient, percussive **Giant**:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — Proclamation to the Trolls
> *"When you go in search of honey, you must expect to be stung by bees. Begone! I shall not take your life, but these folk may not be so forgiving."*

Terrified by the monk of iron, the surviving trolls fled into the deep reeds of the Evermoor marsh. Kaelen Greenbottle packed Tvyordiy's kit with cured meats, sharp cheese, long-keeping bread, and a sealed flask of fiery distilled dwarven spirits. In return, Tvyordiy handed him one of Puck’s calling cards, directing the grateful halfling to seek out Aufox Manor and Puck's laboratory in Leilon for potent alchemical supplies.

### 🌊 The Willow Hermitage & The Yartar Alms

At the quiet confluence where the gray Surbrin emptied into the broad emerald currents of the Dessarin River, Tvyordiy stepped down into the weeping willow grove where his brother [[Myagkiy Znak]] had lived his three-year hermitage before wandering to Waterdeep. 

The autumn leaves had fallen, carpeting the riverbank in bronze and gold. Sitting upon the smoothed hearthstones where Myagkiy had once watched the cold water flow, Tvyordiy held his ancestral Auran coin and prayed silently to the philosophy of **Znak**: for the unyielding hardness of the Hard Sign (Ъ) to hold fast against the sky, and the gentle mercy of the Soft Sign (Ь) to temper that strength in his brother's absence.

Entering the fortified water-city of Yartar in his friar's disguise, he explored the bustling timber wharves and visited **The Rivermaid’s Refuge**, a quiet shelter for orphans along the docks. Without ceremony, he left fresh food and a gleaming stack of **5 Platinum Pieces** in the larder, nodding in silent kinship to a scruffy young urchin before slipping away into the morning fog.

### 🌲 The High Forest Wardens & The Grandfather Tree

At the eastern borders of the Dessarin Valley, the wagon ruts dissolved into spongy moss and tangled roots. The smell of river silt gave way to the deep, primordial scent of ancient spruce, cedar, and crushed needles. Ahead rose the dark, impenetrable wall of the **High Forest**, crowned miles above the canopy by the glinting, snow-capped glaciers of the **Star Mounts**.

As Tvyordiy crossed the forest verge, the ambient chatter of birds went abruptly silent. 

From thirty feet above in the dense cedar canopy came the faint, synchronized clicks of drawn recurve bowstrings. A voice rang out from the shadows in melodic, archaic Elvish:

"Halt, traveler of the road. No wanderer sets foot past the Laughing Hollow without paying tribute to the Wardens of Eaerlann."

Halting with his staff planted lightly in the moss, Tvyordiy tilted his head upward. Invoking his *Tongue of the Sun and Moon*, he replied in the fluid, musical cadence of ancient Eaerlanni Elvish—the holy words of the Forest Queen he had learned from his brother Rhynok:

"Peace, brothers of the forest. Surely you have no need to levy a fine upon a man of meagre means who only wishes to pass through the forest spreading the good word of Mielikki?"

The canopy hung silent for two heartbeats. 

A lithe wood elf ranger in mottled leather and living pine bark dropped soundlessly from the boughs, landing upon the loam without disturbing a leaf. She tilted her head, her golden hawk-eyes scanning his coarse friar's robes with sharp scrutiny:

"Mielikki hears the prayers of all who walk beneath her boughs," she answered smoothly, her tone analytical. "Yet it has been three centuries since a human preacher crossed the Dessarin speaking the high cadence of ancient Eaerlann... with hands so scarred by iron and combat. You walk with the rooted balance of an unyielding oak, traveler, not the shambling gait of a roadside friar. And Our Lady does not ask for gold—she asks for truth. We do not charge tribute in coin; our tribute is knowing what walks within our canopy."

She rested a hand upon her cedar bow:

"Tell us who you truly are and why you seek the high needles, and Eaerlann will grant you safe passage to the foothills."

Tvyordiy smiled gently. Lifting both hands, he took hold of his traveling cap and drew it away. The human illusion dissolved into thin air, revealing his majestic silver-tipped raptor crest, keen golden eyes, and twelve feet of folded gray wings.

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — To the Path-Wardens
> *"Three things cannot hide for long: the Moon, the Sun, and the Truth."*

The wardens' eyes widened in astonishment. Recognizing an elder son of the sacred Star Mounts aeries who carried the blessing of the Forest Queen, the captain and her rangers lowered their bows with deep, formal bows of reverence. 

"The skies have wept for your kind on the high peaks, Master of the Winds," the captain breathed, signaling into the canopy. "Pass freely through the Laughing Hollow. But tread with care: the Tree Ghost Uthgardt are gathered in force at the Grandfather Tree, and the crags above Mount Bardun are stirred by savage wings."

Bowing in return, Tvyordiy pressed deeper into the ancient woods.

### 🌳 The Sacred Clearing & The Shaman's Scent

Deep within the primordial forest, where four-hundred-foot ancient oaks formed a vaulted canopy of living green, Tvyordiy reached the sacred clearing of the **Grandfather Tree**. Ancient **Dryads** of the great tree drifted among the mossy boughs, their translucent, leaf-crowned forms harmonizing with the primordial heartbeat of the woods. 

From the fern groves stepped five painted **Tree Ghost Uthgardt Barbarians**, led by Chief Boorvald Orcbane's daughter Athela—wielding an elven Oathbow—and the blind shaman Elder Kaya.

Elder Kaya halted abruptly. Lifting her scarred, sightless face to the wind, she sniffed the damp air, her weathered brow furrowing in deep perplexity:

"A strange spirit walks the moss," Kaya rasped, her voice vibrating with ancestral authority. "Feathers of the eagle and wings of the boundless sky... yet beneath the plumage runs the dark, subterranean blood-scent of the wild boar. We know the cursed blood of the beast-shifters, stranger. The **Gray Wolves** surrendered their souls to the moon and became butchers in the dark. Tell me: does the beast master your mind, or do you steer its hooves?"

Tvyordiy dropped his hood and bowed low with martial reverence. Speaking in fluent **Bothii**, his subterranean baritone carried across the moss:

"You have the sight. It is something we can only do with intense meditation, and perhaps only grandmasters could do without undergoing a trance. It is true, I am a child of the sky. I have been... cursed with the bite of a creature who could turn from man to boar and... something in between the two. I fought men who could turn into boars but were not the same as these; they had no animal within them. I could have had my brother cleanse me of this, but something made me turn away his healing hands. But where are my manners. I am Tvyordiy Znak of the Order of Znak, a journey to the west in the burgeoning town of Leilon."

He straightened, meeting the blind shaman's gaze with steady, unwavering focus:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — To the Tree Ghosts
> *"Be master of mind rather than mastered by mind."*

A profound silence descended over the sacred hollow. The ancient dryads paused in the high branches, their whisperings hushed; the painted warriors lowered their cedar spears in instinctive reverence before the iron discipline radiating from the monk's center. Unlike the feral Gray Wolves who had fallen to the beast, this traveler held the swine in unbreakable chains of the soul.

Athela lowered her Oathbow, studying the quiet strength in his feathered stance. 

Elder Kaya stepped forward, resting her blackened wooden staff upon the spongy earth. A faint, knowing smile creased her leathery lips:

"You hold your beast in iron chains, bird-monk, yet the chains rattle when the north wind blows. You seek the high peaks, but the winter is closing fast, and Mount Nimbus will shatter hollow bones. Stay with us a while, child of the sky. For all your years of discipline, you have much yet to learn from the earth before you challenge the heavens."

Recognizing the wisdom of the shamans, Tvyordiy accepted their hospitality as the first winter gales began to scream through the high canopy.

### ❄️ The Three-Month Overwintering (Deep Winter, 1500–1501 DR)

As the calendar turned through the waning days of Uktar into the brutal cold of Nightal and Hammer, the northern winter slammed shut like an iron portcullis.

High above the canopy of the High Forest, the towering granite spires of the Star Mounts vanished into a ceaseless, swirling maelstrom of screaming black blizzards. Sub-zero downdrafts tore through the mountain cirques at ninety miles an hour, shearing massive stone cornices from the cliffs and unleashing thunderous avalanches that shook the roots of the forest miles below. 

To attempt an ascent of Mount Nimbus's four-hundred-foot sheer ice chimneys in the dead of winter was not an act of valor; it was suicide. The brittle cold would freeze wing-pinions into glass and tear an Aarakocra from the rock face into the bottomless abyss.

Tvyordiy understood the rhythms of stone and sky. He chose the patient discipline of the earth: for three full months—through the deep freeze of 1500–1501 DR—he overwintered in the sacred hollow of the Grandfather Tree among the Tree Ghost Uthgardt.

#### Elk Hunting with Athela Orcbane
Beneath the cathedral branches of the ancient oaks, five feet of virgin snow muffled the world in profound silence. While his great wings were bound beneath a heavy mantle of grease-rubbed wolf pelts, Tvyordiy found a different freedom on foot. Strapping rawhide-laced willow snowshoes to his talons, he accompanied Chief's daughter Athela Orcbane into the trackless drifts.

Where the barbarians relied on instinct and raw endurance, Tvyordiy moved with the silent, centered weightlessness of the Drunken Master. He learned to read the crust of the snow, tracking the heavy, wandering hoof-prints of the great royal elk through the Laughing Hollow. In the bitter cold, hunting was not sport—it was sacred survival. 

When a massive twelve-point winter bull was brought down at the edge of a frozen stream, Tvyordiy knelt in the snow beside Athela. He placed his bare talon upon the steaming pelt, bowing his head in the Uthgardt manner, whispering his own monastic prayer of thanks to the beast that would keep the tribe's children fed until the spring thaw.

#### Longfire Discussions with Elder Kaya
At night, deep within the hollow root-caverns of the Grandfather Tree, the longfire burned with fragrant birch and dried peat. 

Elder Kaya, her clouded, blind eyes reflecting the glowing coals, sat cross-legged opposite Tvyordiy. She would reach out with withered, soot-stained fingers, tracing the bone ridges of his wrists, feeling the heavy, subterranean pulse running through his blood.

"You speak of the boar within you as a disease, bird-monk," Kaya rasped, her voice dry as winter reeds. "You say you chain it with your *ki*, terrified that its bristles will sprout through your feathers. But you misunderstand the spirits of the earth."

She threw a handful of dried sage onto the embers, filling the hollow with sweet, pungent smoke.

"The bird belongs to the boundless heavens. It is swift, graceful, and unattached. But what happens to the bird when the great blizzard comes? If it cannot root itself, the gale catches its hollow bones and breaks it against the cliff. The Boar does not fear the blizzard. The Boar drops its snout, digs its hooves into the frozen mud, and becomes one with the bedrock. It cannot be moved."

Kaya leaned forward, tapping his breastbone with her blackened staff:

"Your Goliath brother, [[Krageer Stoneface Ogolakanu|Krageer]]—he runs with the Bear. He roars his fury into the axes of his foes. But you... you carry the Boar. It is the unyielding anchor that keeps your sky-spirit from drifting away into nothingness. Do not fight the root, Tvyordiy. Let the boar be your foundation, and your monastic spirit will be the tree that blooms above it."

#### The Dracolich Frost & The Parting Blessing
As the deep winter deepened, Tvyordiy unbuttoned his linen tunic before the longfire, exposing the pale, slate-gray discoloration across his left ribs—the bone-deep necrotic ache that had chilled his flesh since the battle of the Dragon's Mausoleum against [[Chardansearavitriol|Ebondeath]].

Elder Kaya placed her calloused palm against the cold skin. A shadow crossed her weathered face.

"No herb of the forest and no roots of the earth can scour this," Kaya spoke quietly, shaking her braided head. "This is the necrotic rot of an ancient wyrm, touched by the cold hand of a god of the dead. Earthly salves would merely wither against it. There is only one furnace capable of burning away this chill: the primordial gales of the boundless sky itself. The storm that swirls at the apex of Mount Nimbus does not merely blow; it strips the soul down to its purest essence. It will burn the dead dragon's frost from your marrow—or it will freeze your heart forever."

#### Monk Katas in Chest-Deep Snow Drifts
Every morning, long before the winter dawn brought its pale gray light, Tvyordiy stripped off his furs and waded into the chest-deep drifts of the clearing.

The air was thirty below zero, cold enough to shatter steel. His bare talons sank into the packed ice; his breath billowed from his beak in rhythmic, churning clouds of white steam. Moving slowly at first, he ran through the foundational forms of the Drunken Master and the immovable stances of the Iron Root. 

He did not fight the freezing gale whistling through the branches; he breathed it into his dantian, circulating his internal fire (*ki*) through every meridian until his skin steamed against the falling snow. Foot by foot, he carved circular paths through the drifts, his center of gravity unshakeable, his strikes snapping like dry cedar wood. The Hard Sign (**Ъ**) made flesh against the northern frost.

#### The Thaw of Ches & The Parting Gift
By the first days of Ches in 1501 DR, the suffocating blizzards relented, and roaring meltwater began to carve sapphire channels through the snowbanks. 

Strapping his pack tight and taking up his quarterstaff and silvered *Qiang* spear, Tvyordiy prepared to take his leave. Elder Kaya approached, placing a carved bone vial into his talons:

"This is rendered grease from a great winter cave-bear, blended with ghost-lichen," Kaya rasped with a warm, maternal blessing. "Rub it into your sinews before you begin the final chimney of Mount Nimbus. It will keep your blood moving until the sky claims you."

With a deep bow to Athela, Elder Kaya, and the sacred dryads, Tvyordiy turned his face toward the rugged foothills of the Star Mounts.
