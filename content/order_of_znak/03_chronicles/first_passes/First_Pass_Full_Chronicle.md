# 🦅 The Breath of the Boundless Sky: The Saga of Tvyordiy Znak

> **Chronicle Navigation:** ⬅️ **Back to:** [[07 - Epilogue - Bridge Campaign|Epilogue: Bridge Campaign]] | 🏠 [[The Order of Znak Complete Campaign Chronicle|Campaign Chronicle Hub]]

---

*What [[Tvyordiy Znak]] did in the years following the training of the [[The Acolytes of Znak Campaign Plan|Acolytes]], before the gathering that would become the [[07 - Epilogue - Bridge Campaign|Bridge Campaign]].*

When [[The Order of Znak]] scattered after five winters of rebuilding and tutelage in [[Leilon]], they did not depart as an army marching under banner. They left as river water finding separate cracks in stone. [[Krageer Stoneface Ogolakanu]] had turned north toward the cold mountains of his ancestors with [[Cooragh]], leaving behind only the smoke of her cooking fires. Of what became of him in the high country, no word had reached the coast; in those years, the high peaks were a white wall against the sky, keeping their own counsel.

For [[Tvyordiy Znak]] and [[Myagkiy Znak]], the road began where it had always begun: together, upon the parapets of [[Aufox Manor]], looking out over the grey water of the Sea of Swords.

## Chapter 1: The Mark of the Sign

*Table Session: October 27, 2026*  
*In-Game Setting: Autumn, 1500 DR (The Year of the Sea's Secrets Revealed)*  
*Location: Parapet of Aufox Manor, Leilon*

The autumn wind rolling off the Sea of Swords carried a sharp northern frost. Sixty feet below the stone ramparts of [[Aufox Manor]], the gravel courtyard rang with the steady crack of ash wood and the clatter of steel. 

[[Burnice Znak]], nearing twenty summers and standing tall in polished half-plate, reset her guard and drove an arming sword forward in a fluid, disciplined thrust. Her twin brother, [[Smithwell Znak]], red-cheeked and broad across the shoulders, counted time with a deep, rhythmic chant as he swung a weighted wooden greataxe—cadence borrowed straight from Krageer’s fires. Perched upon a rain barrel, young [[Yasmina]] flicked arcs of blue sparks between her fingertips, laughing as lizardfolk envoy [[Graxxoamo]] swept low beneath a spinning quarterstaff strike from [[Shriss]].

Watching from the battlements, [[Myagkiy Znak]] leaned his gauntlets against the weathered stone. Streaks of iron-grey touched his dark beard, though the sunburst medallion of the Life Domain upon his chest caught the afternoon light with unblemished warmth. He reached beneath his breastplate and drew out a folded square of frayed linen, worn soft by twelve years of prayer and sweat. Woven upon it in thick black cord were the two Cyrillic glyphs: **Ъ** and **Ь**.

"Five winters, brother," Myagkiy spoke softly into the gale. "When we pulled Burnice and Smithwell from the kraken's mud, they were shivering children with no name and nowhere to go. Now look at them. They hold the gate better than Neverwinter guardsmen."

He turned his gaze toward Tvyordiy. An Aarakocra matures in three short years and rarely sees thirty winters; at twenty, the silver-white frosting the edges of Tvyordiy’s crest feathers were the marks of an elder among his kind.

"Time moves like a hawk in a dive," Myagkiy murmured. "Sometimes I look at you standing on these stones, and I still see that freezing alleyway behind the *Dolphin's Leap* in Waterdeep's Dock Ward... twelve years ago. A soaking wet stray with broken wing-quills and bleeding knuckles, refusing to drop his club even when dock syndicate toughs pulled blades and clubs. Do you ever think about that night?"

Tvyordiy looked out over the parapet. His deep, resonant baritone broke the wind:

"At first, I thought you were another street tough, looking for an easy score, monetarily or... otherwise. But something resonated in you. A pulse of sorts. And the cloth, even old and tattered then... it pulsed with the same rhythm I sensed in you. And then it started to pulse in me. I was taken aback. At that moment, even surrounded by cutthroats, I knew. I knew it would be alright."

Myagkiy smiled—a quiet, luminous thing that reached his eyes.

"The pulse," the cleric nodded. "Not magic. It was iron recognizing the stone that would sharpen it. The vision had given me two marks in the wilderness: the Hard Sign, that which cannot be bent and absorbs the blow; and the Soft Sign, that which soothes the wound and preserves life. The vision said: *Go to the city where men are ground into dust. Find the other half.* And there you were. A half-starved bird with a broken oar-handle."

### The Memory: Fishgut Alley (Waterdeep, Circa 1488 DR)

![[myagkiy_tvyordiy_fishgut_alley_prelude.jpg]]
*Fishgut Alley, Waterdeep Dock Ward (1488 DR) — Young adult Aarakocra Tvyordiy Znak defending urchin Pip with a broken oar against dock syndicate thugs, under the watchful gaze of hermit cleric Myagkiy Znak.*

The parapets of Leilon dissolved into the freezing, sulfurous rain of Waterdeep's notorious Dock Ward.


It was a dead-end cul-de-sac between leaning timber warehouses. Seven-year-old Tvyordiy—scrawny, soaked to the bone, with clipped primaries and ribs visible beneath sodden down—stood over a damp burlap sack containing four salted herring heads and half a loaf of rye. Four waterfront syndicate thugs had cornered him. 

The ringleader, Bran, spat into the gutter and snapped open a notched scaling knife. *"Drop the sack and the stick, bird-boy. Try and flap those ugly wings, and we’ll turn you into soup."*

In his pocket, young Tvyordiy’s fingers brushed his sole inheritance: a cool, feather-light metal disc with an ancient whorl inscribed in **Auran**, the only token left by parents he had never known.

Tvyordiy raised his three-foot ash oar-handle high in his right hand, feigning a frantic overhead smash. 

> **Table Action (Level 0 Urchin):**  
> *Sleight of Hand (Urchin):* `1d20 + 4` = **17** vs. Bran's Passive Insight (10).  
> *Attack (Improvised Ash Club with Advantage):* `[14, 2] + 2` = **16 vs. AC 11 (Hit!)**  
> *Damage:* `1d4 + 2` = **5 bludgeoning damage.**

The club flipped behind his back from right hand to left in a flash of street-taught agility. Bran raised his knife high to parry the empty right hand—and the heavy ash blade in Tvyordiy’s left swung low in a brutal horizontal arc. 

*CRACK.*

Bran’s jaw broke under the impact. The brute dropped to the cobbles in a spray of blood and teeth. Lefty swung an iron belaying pin, grazing Tvyordiy's left wing-shoulder for 2 damage, while Garr's brass knuckles missed, crunching loudly into the rain-slick brickwork.

Refusing to yield an inch of stone, Tvyordiy pivoted on his talons:

> **Table Action (Level 0):**  
> *Club Attack vs. Lefty:* `1d20 + 2` = **18 vs. AC 11 (Hit!)** &rarr; **6 bludgeoning damage.** *(Lefty collapsed into the rain barrels!)*  
> *Off-Hand Talon Swipe vs. Prone Bran:* `[13, 9] + 2` = **15 vs. AC 11 (Hit!)** &rarr; **1 slashing damage.**

Lefty buckled into the barrels, gasping for breath. Bran scrambled backward through the muck, shrieking as Tvyordiy’s talons raked his arm. 

As Garr lunged from the blind side, the dark alley exploded in golden radiance. A column of sacred celestial fire roared down through the downpour (*Sacred Flame*), scorching Garr’s sleeve and driving the brawler back in terror. 

Out of the sea fog stepped a solitary figure in ragged homespun, holding up an open hand that glowed with gentle amber light. *"Drop your weapons and leave this child,"* the hermit commanded. Garr grabbed the whimpering Pip and hauled their wounded comrades into the mist.

Myagkiy sank to one knee in the filth, coming eye-to-eye with the scowling young Aarakocra. He extended a small, steaming loaf of barley bread.

*"You want a piece of me, old man?!"* young Tvyordiy snarled, puffing his wet feathers and brandishing his broken oar. *"I could have taken those toughs! And I am no child—I am ten years old! An adult! I need no help!"*

> **Table Action (Level 0):**  
> *Sleight of Hand (Bread Snatch):* `Natural 20 + 4` = **24!**

In the blink of an eye, the warm loaf vanished from Myagkiy’s palm, tucked deep into the boy's oversized trench coat beside his Auran coin. 

Myagkiy had thrown back his head and laughed with deep, ringing joy. *"Ten years old! Forgive me, venerable master of the docks."* Gently, the hermit laid his hand upon Tvyordiy's bruised wing, channeling *Cure Wounds* (8 HP) until the bone-deep ache vanished forever. *"You have the Hard Sign in your marrow, boy. But together, we will never face it alone."*

### The Dreadnaught’s Whisper & The Dual Calling

The salt wind of Leilon blew the memory clean. 

Beside Tvyordiy, Myagkiy folded the cloth away and rested his hands upon his war belt. 

"Twelve years," Myagkiy said quietly. "And now the circle turns. I called you up here, Tvyordiy, because the Signs are moving again. In my prayers at Lathander’s shrine, the light has felt cold. The cult of **[[Myrkul]]** did not die with Ularan Mortus. Pale remnants and necromantic cabals are stirring in the deep crypts across the Sword Coast. The dead do not rest, brother. Undeath is a freezing cage where souls are starved of the dawn. The Soft Sign in my chest demands that I go. I must seek out these poisoned graves, lay the dead to rest, and cleanse the earth."

Myagkiy turned his gaze upon his brother. "And you. Ever since that dreadnaught sank on the Sea of Swords... tell me what the wind whispered to you that day."

Tvyordiy looked out toward the western horizon where the storm clouds gathered over the deep water. He hesitated, his voice trembling faintly with old awe:

"I... I never spoke of it. Even to this day, Myagkiy, your insight to know what I am not saying astounds me."

Tvyordiy sighed, his shoulders rising with the sea breeze. 

"They spoke to me. You and the others wouldn't have known; you heard the throng of battle, the rush of the storm. To you, the whipping winds of those Myrmidons would have sounded like wind whistling through bamboo leaves. But to me..."

Tvyordiy looked squarely at his brother. He drew in a deep, diaphragmatic breath and exhaled a punctuated, rolling bellow—the harsh, rolling syllables of native **Auran**. It rumbled across the battlements like distant thunder across a mountain pass.

"Talos. He spoke of Talos. Not just that the Destroyer was there in our midst, but that he was elsewhere. In the air itself. When that Myrmidon spoke, it sounded like wind hitting a stone wall and forcing itself over. It had been ripped from its home in the Endless Blue, bound into storm-iron armor, and commanded to kill. There was a terrible resistance in it. And when Deloz struck the killing blow that day, it whispered in its final breath... *'Remember.'*"

Myagkiy lowered his head. "A child of the pure sky, enslaved by storm-iron. That dying spirit whispered to you because you are the only one who can carry the unbending sign into the sky itself—to seek where Talos has poisoned the winds, and break the cage from within."

### The Passing of the Keys

The heavy oak door to the parapet swung open with a creak of iron hinges. 

Stepping out onto the windy roof was **[[Leopold]]**, impeccably dressed in his charcoal woolen surcoat, holding the brass-bound leather ledger of Aufox Manor under his arm. Clustered behind him at the landing were the five grown Acolytes: Burnice, Smithwell, Yasmina, Graxxoamo, and Shriss.

Leopold bowed his head with crisp dignity. "Master Tvyordiy. Master Myagkiy. The provisioning ledgers are settled. The palisades stand firm." He looked at their packed traveling kits. "The horses are not saddled, my lords. Your destinations lie where hooves cannot tread."

Burnice stepped forward, her hand dropping to her sword hilt. "Uncle Tvyordiy? Uncle Myagkiy? Are you... leaving Leilon?"

Leopold quickly raised a white-gloved hand in silent rebuke, cautioning the young warrior against speaking out of turn before the masters. 

Tvyordiy smiled warmly down at the young paladin, playfully waving aside the majordomo's formality:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak
> *"A fish who does not open its mouth gets no tasty worm, but you must beware the hook!"*

In a blur of martial speed, Tvyordiy caught Burnice with a gentle, hooked forearm, enfolding his broad gray wings completely around her as had become his lifelong habit when greeting or parting with his wards.

"In any case, child, a storm makes trees take deeper roots," Tvyordiy rumbled warmly against her hair. "You five can take care of Leilon. The Storm Lord's wrath may have broken against the swords of the coast, but he has left his mark in other places."

He released her with a firm pat on her armored shoulder, exchanging handshakes and steady grips with Smithwell, Yasmina, and the lizardfolk envoys, shepherding the young champions toward the warm hearth below.

At the threshold, Myagkiy placed a heavy gauntlet upon Leopold's shoulder. 

"Leo, you will have the keep," Myagkiy said gently. "I don't know when we will return this time. It is not a quick jaunt to the Wayside Inn for Cooragh's table of treats."

Leopold swallowed hard, his white-gloved hand tightening around the heavy iron ring of estate keys. "But sir... what if...?"

Tvyordiy stepped beside his brother and looked the majordomo squarely in the eye.

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak
> *"Leopold, there isn't enough darkness in all the world to snuff out the light of even one candle. So long as one remains to hold true the Znak, all will be well."*

The majordomo drew himself up to his full height. The anxiety vanished from his eyes, replaced by unshakeable resolve. 

"Then Aufox Manor will stand, Master Tvyordiy," Leopold bowed with formal grace. "Until the call comes."

Before descending, Tvyordiy reached into his tunic and withdrew two thick, vellum missives sealed with the blue wax stamp of Aufox Manor, handing them into Leopold's care. Krageer had already turned north with Cooragh toward the high country months prior, and Rhynok had slipped quietly into the shadowed trails of the wilderness on his own solitary hunt. 

"In the map-room lockbox, Leopold," Tvyordiy instructed softly. "If the seasons turn and the snows melt without our return, deliver them should our brothers ever ride back through the gates."

Both letters detailed their destinations and what the Order must do should either fail to return. But at the close of his missive to [[Krageer Stoneface Ogolakanu]], Tvyordiy had penned a final, unyielding line of warrior wisdom:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — Letter to Krageer
> *"Greater in battle than the man who would conquer a thousand-thousand men, is he who would conquer just one: himself."*

And to [[Rhynok]], the silent shadow-stalker of the woods, Tvyordiy had closed with the patient reassurance of an old traveler:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — Letter to Rhynok
> *"Over every mountain there is a path, although it may not be seen from the valley."*

### 🧪 Munitions in Leilon: Puck & Jack Torver

Before parting ways at the town gates, Tvyordiy descended into the bustling reconstruction square of Leilon to secure his provisions. 

He first sought out the eccentric workshop of **Puck**, the ingenious gnome alchemist and longtime friend to the Order of Znak. Knowing the treacherous, unmapped wilderness that lay across the continent, Tvyordiy stepped amidst the bubbling alembics and ticking brass contraptions.

"Puck, my friend," Tvyordiy rumbled warmly. "I go now on a long journey, and ever have you been a steadfast friend to the Order. I stand in need of your finest restorative draughts—something that can mend broken bone and torn sinew when the road turns cold."

The gnome beamed, bustling behind his shelves and emerging with three thick, crystal vials sealed with stamped silver wax: two *Potions of Greater Healing* and a rare, concentrated *Potion of Superior Healing*. 

"For you, Master Monk, and for the defense of the realm: eight hundred gold pieces for the lot," Puck stated with a proud nod.

Without revealing an inch of the immense fortune nestled within the extradimensional folds of his *Bag of Holding*, Tvyordiy’s clawed fingers slipped inside and quietly withdrew eight neat, heavy stacks of freshly minted gold coins. He set them upon the counter with quiet, unassuming precision. 

"Leopold has charge of Aufox Manor," Tvyordiy smiled as the gnome wrapped the glass vials in soft flannel. "Keep an eye on the kids... err, young men and women. They grow so fast."

Tvyordiy’s beak curved in a subtle, amused smile. "I will do the usual advertising for you if you wish to give me a small stock of business cards. I don't know how far I may roam this time, Master Puck, but I hope to give you a fair share of customers even more exotic than our neighbors in the mere."

Puck froze mid-step, his pointed ears instantly flushing with bright pink pride and sheer fluster. 

"Business cards?!" Puck stammered, caught completely off-guard and flustered with excitement. 

He scrambled frantically beneath the counter, knocking over an empty brass scale with a loud clatter before popping back up, triumphant, with a neat stack of stiff vellum slips tied in emerald silk. Each card was stamped with a silver-leaf cornucopia of Tymora and penned in tidy elven script: *Puck Caribdas, Alchemist & Apothecary — Endorsed by The Order of Znak.*

"More exotic than the Mere?!" Puck laughed nervously, handing the bundle across the counter with two shaking hands. "By the Lady, Master Tvyordiy, if you bring planar travelers or cloud giants down to Leilon to buy healing wash, I will dedicate an entire wing of the shop to your name! Go with Tymora's favor. I will check on Leopold and the young ones every tenday—I swear it!"

Tvyordiy tucked the calling cards securely into his tunic with a deep nod of thanks. 

Crossing the square to **Torver's Post**, Tvyordiy dropped fifteen gold pieces upon the counter for [[Jack Torver]]. Knowing the high granite spires of the Star Mounts would test mortal sinew, he secured heavy hempen climbing ropes, steel pitons, ice crampons, a collapsible ten-foot pole, and a thick, wool-lined traveling cloak to brace against northern sleet. 

With his kit packed tight and his friar's disguise adjusted, Tvyordiy and Myagkiy clasped forearms at the crossroads, turning their faces toward their separate destinies.

## Chapter 2: The Ascent of the Star Mounts

The road eastward from the Sword Coast stretched across four hundred miles of changing earth. 

Tvyordiy Znak traveled at a measured, unhurried pace. While the elemental pull in his marrow was absolute, he had contemplated this departure for five years within the peaceful walls of [[Aufox Manor]]. True discipline was not measured in frantic haste, but in the unyielding cadence of each deliberate step. 

Leaving the salt air of [[Leilon]] behind, he turned north along the High Road.

### 🍻 The Wayside Inn & The Hearth of Phandalin (Late Autumn, 1500 DR)

His first waypoint was the **Wayside Inn**, standing stalwart at the crossroads where the High Road met the Triboar Trail. Entering the common room beneath the modest guise of his friar's cowl, he was greeted by the rich scents of roasting mutton and spiced ale. Innkeeper Martisha and the road travelers bustled about the tables in safety—a far cry from the days when undead cultists and storm wraiths besieged the taproom. Taking a quiet corner, Tvyordiy shared a humble bowl of stew, remembering Cooragh’s boisterous laughter and the feasts of the Order. The realm was at peace, its roads secure because of the blood they had spilled.

Turning east onto the Triboar Trail along the northern foothills of the Sword Mountains, the mountain scrub turned to russet and gold. Before embarking on the lonely eastern highways, Tvyordiy turned his steps down the familiar cart-road toward **[[Phandalin]]**. 

A decade had passed since the young companions of the Order had first arrived in the frontier mining settlement with mud on their boots and copper in their pockets. Standing on the southern ridge, Tvyordiy lowered the coarse friar's cowl of his *Hat of Disguise* and smiled. The burned timber ruins and dilapidated cottages of the old frontier were long gone. In their place stood a robust, prosperous community of dressed granite, sturdy split-rail fences, and neatly stacked cordwood. The chimneys of the smelters coughed gray plumes into the crisp afternoon sky, and the distant ringing of pickaxes echoed from the hills.

He walked down the main lane without guise. In Phandalin, an Aarakocra monk was no curious stranger—he was living history.

Pushing through the heavy oak door of the **[[Stonehill Inn]]**, the blast of warmth from the central stone hearth greeted him, smelling richly of roasted turnips, braised boar ribs, and spiced blackberry cider. 

Behind the heavy counter, innkeeper [[Toblen Stonehill]] was polishing an earthenware tankard. He looked up, his cloth freezing mid-wipe. His ruddy face broke into an astonished, ear-to-ear grin.

"By the gods... Trilena! Pip! Drop those buckets!" Toblen roared into the kitchen, slamming the tankard down. *"Master Tvyordiy has come back!"*

The taproom fell dead silent for two heartbeats before erupting into a clamor of scraping bench-legs and excited shouts. Miners with rock-dust ground into their knuckles, weavers from the hillside farms, and carters in heavy sheepskin coats surged toward the hearth. 

"Ten winters, Master Monk!" bellowed an old prospector named Gurn, his white whiskers bristling as he hoisted a brimming mug of dark stout. "I still tell my grand-lads about the day on [[Icespire Hold]]! When that white devil **[[Cryovain]]** came screaming out of the blizzard, freezing our pack-mules where they stood! We were shivering in our hides, waiting for the frost-breath to turn our marrow to slush—and there you came! Bounded straight across the dragon's snout, talons flashing, and landed a single, thunderous palm right between its horned eyes!"

Gurn slammed his palm against the rough oak table with a resounding *THWACK*.

"Rattled the beast's brains right down into its tail! Stiff as a dried salt-cod in mid-air! Smashed down onto the mountain courtyard like a sack of river rocks! That was the blow that broke the winter, lads! Drink to the Order of Znak!"

The common room roared in approval, raising cups high. 

Tvyordiy accepted a bowl of piping hot potato leek soup and a cup of roasted dandelion tea, sitting cross-legged upon a low bench near the hearthstones. He listened with quiet eyes, his gravelly baritone rumbling with easy warmth:

> [!NOTE] Aphorisms of Tvyordiy Znak — At the Stonehill Hearth
> *"A well-traveled road is made not by the feet of one giant, but by the steady walking of many common folk. The dragon had great wings and terrible teeth, yet here you sit, tending your hearths and feeding your children. That is the true victory over monsters."*

When Gurn and the miners pressed pouches of raw silver nuggets and frontier coin into his hands, Tvyordiy gently pushed them back across the wood. "Give it to the masonry guild for the town wall. An unbending stone is worth more than silver when the north wind blows."

Before leaving, he paid a quiet visit to **Barthen's Provisions**. [[Elmar Barthen]], now stooped with age but with the same shrewd, honest gleam in his eye, gripped Tvyordiy’s taloned forearm with both hands.

"Our trade wagons ride the road to Leilon in peace because of you and Myagkiy, brother," Barthen rasped softly. "Take this on the high trails." 

The merchant loaded his pack with smoked venison strips, hard travel biscuits, a wheel of sharp cheddar, and a tin of pure beeswax to buff his quarterstaff. With a deep bow and a protective sweep of his wings enfolding the old shopkeeper, Tvyordiy turned eastward onto the Triboar Trail toward Triboar.

### 🐎 A Week in Triboar: Apple Brandy & The Creeping Mist

East of Phandalin, the rolling foothills flattened into the vast, open plains of the Dessarin Valley. At the meeting of the Long Road and the Triboar Trail lay **Triboar**, sprawling across the prairie like a resting beast of burden.

Here, Tvyordiy drew the coarse friar's cowl of his *Hat of Disguise* low over his brow, choosing to linger for a full week. While his elemental destination called to his marrow, the discipline of the Hard Sign demanded that a traveler understand the earth he walked upon before attempting to master the sky. 

Triboar was a town of horses, riverboats, and autumn harvests. In the lively common room of the **Northshield House**, Tvyordiy took a quiet corner table beneath the cedar beams. He savored the hearty simple fare of the frontier: sweet, pan-fried river trout pulled fresh from the emerald waters of the Dessarin, warm rye loaves, and cups of crisp, in-season apple brandy freshly pressed from local valley orchards. 

Being barely fifty leagues down the cart-road from Phandalin and Leilon, the exploits of the **Order of Znak** were legendary here. From behind his modest friar's illusion, Tvyordiy listened with quiet, gleaming eyes as carters, teamsters, and wandering merchants praised the heroes of Aufox Manor over foaming tankards. They toasted the giants and monks who had broken the dragon Cryovain and shattered the storm-cults, allowing trade to flow freely from the coast to the valley. There was a profound, serene satisfaction in hearing one's life's work celebrated by common folk who had no idea the towering raptor hero sat right beside them, quietly sipping cider.

At the market stalls of the Lion's Share, Tvyordiy stocked his traveling pack with cold-weather provisions. When he purchased three large, cloth-wrapped blocks of pure rendered beef and mutton suet, the provisioner wiped his hands on his apron and raised an incredulous eyebrow:

"Three whole slabs of suet, Brother?" the merchant chuckled good-naturedly. "You must really have a soft heart for the winter songbirds! They'll sing your praises all the way to Waterdeep."

Tvyordiy’s beak curved beneath his illusion, offering a serene, pious nod: "All creatures of the sky require fat against the freezing north, good sir." In truth, the dense, high-calorie suet was essential fuel for an Aarakocra's searing avian metabolism when ascending the freezing upper troposphere.

Yet beneath the town’s autumn cheer ran an unsettling undercurrent. 

In the late evenings, as drovers gathered close around the tavern fire-pits, the talk turned hushed and uneasy. Caravan scouts down from the Evermoor fringes and the lonely eastern crossroads whispered of unnatural phenomena on the high roads. Travelers spoke of bizarre, bone-chilling white fogs that did not burn away with the morning sun—dense mists that rolled unnaturally against the prevailing wind, smelling of damp earth and old graves. A family of Vistani tinkers had passed through with dark warnings, and two separate supply wagons heading toward the eastern passes had vanished entirely inside the fog, leaving behind not a wheel-rut, a dead mule, or a solitary footprint in the dust. 

Tvyordiy made mental note of the warnings, feeling the cold weight of the signs upon his chest. 

At the end of the tenday, refreshed in body and grounded in the quiet pulse of the land, Tvyordiy packed his kit, settled his modest tab with a silver piece, and set out past the eastern stockade onto the desolate, rain-slick mud of the Evermoor Way.

### ⚔️ The Evermoor Way Ambush & The Hard Sign's Proclamation

Two leagues west of the Surbrin crossing, black marsh smoke rose above the wagon ruts. 

A river trading caravan had been shattered across the mud. Three hulking brutes—two slimy River Trolls and a nine-foot, lichen-crusted Moss Troll—had overturned the baggage wain, cornering halfling merchant Kaelen Greenbottle and two wounded guards against the sideboard.

Breaking from the tree-line skimming inches above the earth at seventy feet of flight speed, Tvyordiy dropped his disguise:
* Channeling his *Circlet of Blasting*, three incandescent beams of *Scorching Ray* streaked through the mist, igniting the trolls' hide and disabling their unnatural regeneration.
* Whirling his *Vicious Quarterstaff +2*, he struck the lead river troll with a concussive blow that locked its nerves in a brutal *Stunning Strike*.
* Executing the fluid, swaying redirection of the Drunken Master, he disengaged without a scratch, vaulted forty feet across the road ruts, and battered the massive Moss Troll into the wagon boards with his fists.

When the Moss Troll raked its jagged talons across his chest, the nonmagical claws scraped harmlessly off his unyielding wereboar hide without breaking the skin. Tvyordiy did not flinch:

> *"Light some torches and attack from the rear. Fire is their foe."*

Rallying behind his iron calm, the guards thrust burning pitch torches into the beast, while Tvyordiy brought his quarterstaff down in a concussive *Crusher* critical strike that pinned the moss troll to the timbers until the guards reduced it to ash. 

Drawing his slender wand from Thunder Cliffs, Tvyordiy unleashed an eight-dart, 6th-level volley from his *Wand of Magic Missile*, obliterating the charging river troll's momentum in a staccato of force explosions before dropping into a rooted *Patient Defense*. Reeling on shattered limbs at eleven hit points, the surviving river trolls cowered in the ditch.

Tvyordiy planted the butt of his staff in the gravel, his subterranean baritone cutting through the smoke:

> **"I am Tvyordiy Znak of the Order of Znak. That is the order of Znak, not the chaos of Znak."**

Invoking *Tongue of the Sun and Moon*, his voice rumbled in ancient, percussive **Giant**:

> **"When you go in search of honey, you must expect to be stung by bees. Begone, I shall not take your life, but these folk may not be so forgiving."**

Terrified by the monk of iron, the surviving trolls fled into the deep reeds of the Evermoor marsh. Kaelen Greenbottle packed Tvyordiy's kit with cured meats, sharp cheese, long-keeping bread, and a sealed flask of fiery distilled dwarven spirits. ### ☁️ Into the Azure Abyss

Crossing the sapphire threshold of the Mount Nimbus vortex was like falling upward into eternity.

There was no earth beneath their feet, no vaulted heavens above—only an inexhaustible, shadowless azure stretching across boundless leagues. Vast archipelagoes of floating emerald earthmotes, ancient elven sky-ruins, and wandering cumulus reefs drifted along gentle, warm thermal rivers. Plumes of crystalline water poured from hanging precipices, tumbling through miles of open sky before dispersing into shimmering rainbow mist. The air tasted of pure sunlight, cool rain, and untamed freedom—a breath unburdened by mortal soot or decay.

For [[Yeri]], the crossing was nothing short of divine transfiguration. Her four gossamer dragonfly wings maintained a high, melodic thrum, scattering prismatic starlight across the slipstream. She banked, spiraled, and climbed in effortless arcs, testing the buoyant currents with breathless, staccato whistles. For centuries, her Kenku kin had been bound to the mud, mourning stolen wings in back-alley gutters. Now, unbound from gravity, she laughed—a sharp, trilling caw that rang like silver bells across the endless blue.

For [[Tvyordiy Znak]], however, the plane did not merely welcome him—it recognized him.

Beneath his traveling robes, the silver disc pressed against his chest grew searingly warm. The ancestral Auran coin vibrated with a deep harmonic frequency, matching the rhythm of his own pulse. As he flared his massive raptor wings, spanning twelve feet of gray-and-white plumage, the air currents did not resist him; they bore him aloft with the instinctive familiarity of a mother’s embrace. His newly crystallized *Diamond Soul* filtered the pure elemental air, scouring away the last remnants of Toril’s grime.

Following the persistent pull of the coin, Tvyordiy peered south-southeast across fifteen leagues of open sky. There, perched atop a colossal, spiraling earthmote crowned in ancient silver spires and wind-carved perches, stood **The Aerie of the Boundless Whorl**—the legendary redoubt of his lineage.

Yet the horizon was not peaceful. 

To the far north, the serene azure curdled into a bruised, necrotic violet. A monstrous wall of thunderheads churned against the trade winds, reeking of ozone, burnt copper, and rotting sea-kelp. Jagged sheets of purple lightning split the roiling clouds—the unmistakable, corruptive tempest of [[Talos]] the Destroyer, creeping like a gangrene across the eternal blue.

His incisors ached, lengthening against his lower beak; coarse black bristles prickled beneath his breast feathers, and a red veil of feral rage flashed across his vision. Her feathers were ruffled, her dark eyes wide as the titanic predator wheeled toward their quadrant.

*No,* Tvyordiy told himself, clenching his talons into fists until the knuckles cracked. *Not here. Not in this pure sky. She climbed through ice and fire to find the stars; she will not see me become the swine.*

### 🏛️ The Spires of the Boundless Whorl & The Silver Tessera

### 🪽 The Clutch-Twin: Klee'errk (Aial Sun-Catcher)

When the Talosite raid besieged the Lower Perch, one egg was too fragile to move—its shell bore a hairline fracture from the concussive thunder-stones, unable to survive the violent planar rift. ### 📜 The Sky-Weaver's Revelation: Clan Matron Soolin & The True Name

At the center of the hall, seated upon a throne of woven cedar, sat **Clan Matron Soolin**.

She was ancient—well over a hundred summers old, a venerable matriarch whose life spanned generations of sky-weavers. Her crest feathers were wispy and silver as cirrus clouds, and her clouded, milky eyes were blind. "Bring him forward," the Matron rasped, her voice like dry autumn leaves rustling before a gale. "Bring Vaelen's lost boy to me."

Let the boar be your foundation, and your monastic spirit will be the tree that blooms above it."

#### The Dracolich Frost & The Parting Blessing
As the deep winter deepened, Tvyordiy unbuttoned his linen tunic before the longfire, exposing the pale, slate-gray discoloration across his left ribs—the bone-deep necrotic ache that had chilled his flesh since the battle of the Dragon's Mausoleum against [[Chardansearavitriol|Ebondeath]].

Elder Kaya placed her calloused palm against the cold skin. A shadow crossed her weathered face.

"No herb of the forest and no roots of the earth can scour this," Kaya spoke quietly, shaking her braided head. "This is the necrotic rot of an ancient wyrm, touched by the cold hand of a god of the dead. Earthly salves would merely wither against it. There is only one furnace capable of burning away this chill: the primordial gales of the boundless sky itself. The storm that swirls at the apex of Mount Nimbus does not merely blow; it strips the soul down to its purest essence. It will burn the dead dragon's frost from your marrow—or it will freeze your heart forever."

#### Monk Katas in Chest-Deep Snow Drifts
Every morning, long before the winter dawn brought its pale gray light, Tvyordiy stripped off his furs and waded into the chest-deep drifts of the clearing.

The air was thirty below zero, cold enough to shatter steel. His bare talons sank into the packed ice; his breath billowed from his beak in rhythmic, churning clouds of white steam. Moving slowly at first, he ran through the foundational forms of the Drunken Master and the immovable stances of the Iron Root. 

He did not fight the freezing gale whistling through the branches; he breathed it into his dantian, circulating his internal fire (*ki*) through every meridian until his skin steamed against the falling snow. Foot by foot, he carved circular paths through the drifts, his center of gravity unshakeable, his strikes snapping like dry cedar wood. The Hard Sign (**Ъ**) made flesh against the northern frost.

#### The Thaw of Ches & The Parting Gift
By the first days of Ches in 1501 DR, the suffocating blizzards relented, and roaring meltwater began to carve sapphire channels through the snowbanks. 

Strapping his pack tight and taking up his quarterstaff and silvered *Qiang* spear, Tvyordiy prepared to take his leave. Elder Kaya approached, placing a carved bone vial into his talons:

"This is rendered grease from a great winter cave-bear, blended with ghost-lichen," Kaya rasped with a warm, maternal blessing. "Rub it into your sinews before you begin the final chimney of Mount Nimbus. It will keep your blood moving until the sky claims you."

With a deep bow to Athela, Elder Kaya, and the sacred dryads, Tvyordiy turned his face toward the rugged foothills of the Star Mounts.

## Chapter 3: The Boundless Sky

*Table Session: October 28, 2026*  
*In-Game Setting: Autumn, 1500 DR (The Year of the Sea's Secrets Revealed)*  
*Location: The Elemental Plane of Air (The Cloud-Reefs & The Aerie of the Boundless Whorl)*

---

### ☁️ Into the Azure Abyss

Crossing the sapphire threshold of the Mount Nimbus vortex was like falling upward into eternity.

There was no earth beneath their feet, no vaulted heavens above—only an inexhaustible, shadowless azure stretching across boundless leagues. Vast archipelagoes of floating emerald earthmotes, ancient elven sky-ruins, and wandering cumulus reefs drifted along gentle, warm thermal rivers. Plumes of crystalline water poured from hanging precipices, tumbling through miles of open sky before dispersing into shimmering rainbow mist. The air tasted of pure sunlight, cool rain, and untamed freedom—a breath unburdened by mortal soot or decay.

For [[Yeri]], the crossing was nothing short of divine transfiguration. Her four gossamer dragonfly wings maintained a high, melodic thrum, scattering prismatic starlight across the slipstream. She banked, spiraled, and climbed in effortless arcs, testing the buoyant currents with breathless, staccato whistles. For centuries, her Kenku kin had been bound to the mud, mourning stolen wings in back-alley gutters. Now, unbound from gravity, she laughed—a sharp, trilling caw that rang like silver bells across the endless blue.

For [[Tvyordiy Znak]], however, the plane did not merely welcome him—it recognized him.

Beneath his traveling robes, the silver disc pressed against his chest grew searingly warm. The ancestral Auran coin vibrated with a deep harmonic frequency, matching the rhythm of his own pulse. As he flared his massive raptor wings, spanning twelve feet of gray-and-white plumage, the air currents did not resist him; they bore him aloft with the instinctive familiarity of a mother’s embrace. His newly crystallized *Diamond Soul* filtered the pure elemental air, scouring away the last remnants of Toril’s grime.

Following the persistent pull of the coin, Tvyordiy peered south-southeast across fifteen leagues of open sky. There, perched atop a colossal, spiraling earthmote crowned in ancient silver spires and wind-carved perches, stood **The Aerie of the Boundless Whorl**—the legendary redoubt of his lineage.

Yet the horizon was not peaceful. 

To the far north, the serene azure curdled into a bruised, necrotic violet. A monstrous wall of thunderheads churned against the trade winds, reeking of ozone, burnt copper, and rotting sea-kelp. Jagged sheets of purple lightning split the roiling clouds—the unmistakable, corruptive tempest of [[Talos]] the Destroyer, creeping like a gangrene across the eternal blue.

---

### 🐗 The Patrol of the Tempest Vanguard

A hunting wing broke from the vanguard of the northern storm.

Riding the front edge of a squall came a massive **Storm Roc**, its plumage crackling with arcs of violet electricity, its wingspan blotting out the sunlit cloud-banks below. Bolted to its spine was an iron war-saddle holding two **Anchorites of Talos**—half-orc storm shamans clad in greasy studded leather, their faces hidden behind bleached, snarling wild boar skulls.

Across the gulf of air rolled the scream of a war horn: a harsh, porcine squeal magnified by unnatural thunder.

The sound struck Tvyordiy like an iron mallet to the chest.

In his marrow, something ancient, filthy, and primal jerked awake. The dormant wereboar lycanthropy—contracted years ago during the bloody defense of the Sword Coast and locked beneath twelve years of monastic meditation—surged against its bonds. His incisors ached, lengthening against his lower beak; coarse black bristles prickled beneath his breast feathers, and a red veil of feral rage flashed across his vision. The beast smelled the boar-totem shamans and demanded blood, urging him to drop into a reckless dive and tear the riders throat from throat.

Beside him, Yeri hovered on rapidly beating wings, her small chest heaving. She had expended nearly her entire arcane reserve upon the frozen spire of Mount Nimbus—disintegrating avalanches, burning sorcery points to twin life-saving wards, and blasting the frost-gale titan into the chasm. Her feathers were ruffled, her dark eyes wide as the titanic predator wheeled toward their quadrant.

*No,* Tvyordiy told himself, clenching his talons into fists until the knuckles cracked. *Not here. Not in this pure sky. She climbed through ice and fire to find the stars; she will not see me become the swine.*

---

### 🌫️ The Veiled Descent: Evasion Behind the Cataract

Tvyordiy caught Yeri’s eye and gave a sharp, downward slice of his claw: *break line of sight. Dive.*

Understanding instantly, Yeri touched two fingers to her temple, murmuring a soft, urgent invocation:
> *"May the quiet path open..."* (*Guidance* cast upon herself).

Tucking his broad wings close to his torso, Tvyordiy surrendered to gravity, dropping into a sheer, whistling plunge at seventy-five feet per round. Yeri angled her iridescent gossamer wings, trailing directly in his slipstream like a darting dragonfly.

Four thousand feet below, an isolated earthmote hung adrift, crowned with weeping silver ferns. From its northern shelf, an aerial cataract roared into the void—a curtain of foaming, thunderous water cascading into infinite space.

> **Table Action (Aerial Stealth & Evasion):**  
> *Yeri (Ы):* Stealth check with *Guidance* (+1d4) &rarr; `1d20 + 2 + 3` = **16 vs. Storm Roc Passive Perception (15) — Success!**  
> *Tvyordiy (Ъ):* Dexterity (Stealth) with *Diamond Soul* grace &rarr; `1d20 + 4` = **16 vs. Storm Roc Passive Perception (15) — Success!**

Slicing through the cold spray, Yeri slipped behind the roaring water, pressing her back against the mossy basalt and timing the hum of her dragonfly wings to the crashing timbre of the falls. A heartbeat later, Tvyordiy swept in beside her, folding his twelve-foot wings around himself and freezing into the immobility of carved stone.

*WHOOSH.*

A hurricane gust hammered the rock face. The colossal shadow of the Storm Roc swept across the translucent curtain of water, its massive talons churning the foam mere yards from their hiding place. The reek of brimstone, ozone, and wet boar-hide washed through the spray.

From atop the mount, an Anchorite raised the boar-skull horn and blew a second time—a low, inquiring grunt that vibrated through the stone and set Tvyordiy’s teeth on edge.

Behind the cataract, Tvyordiy’s muscles locked like iron bands. The wild beast within his chest roared to answer the challenge, thrashing against the cage of his *ki*. His breath came in ragged, wheezing gasps, his golden raptor eyes dilating with feral frenzy.

Then, a small, feather-light hand pressed firmly against his wrist.

Yeri did not speak. She simply stood beside him in the mist, her dark eyes clear, steady, and entirely unafraid. Her small talons were cool against his burning skin, anchoring him to the present, to the brotherhood of the Sign.

Tvyordiy closed his eyes. He drew in the moisture of the falls, exhaling the heat through his nostrils, repeating the foundational kata of the Weeping Willow:
> *"Be master of mind rather than mastered by mind."*

The iron bars held. The crimson tide receded into the shadowed recesses of his marrow.

Above, finding no purchase in the deafening cataract and seeing only empty clouds, the Talosite shamans wrenched their mount's bit. With an angry, crackling shriek, the Storm Roc wheeled north, banking back toward the gathering siege.

---

### 🏛️ The Spires of the Boundless Whorl & The Silver Tessera

When the ozone tang finally cleared from the thermal currents, Tvyordiy and Yeri emerged from behind the falls.

Catching the rising updraft, they crossed the remaining leagues of open air, guided by the insistent warmth of the ancestral coin. As they approached the soaring spires of the **Boundless Whorl**, the true grandeur of the aerie unfolded before them. Carved from iridescent cloudstone and native white granite, the citadel rose in concentric tiers, connected not by stairways or ladders, but by soaring open-air landings, launching balconies, and wind-sculpted perches open to the four quarters of heaven. The air sang with the harmonic whistling of wind-flutes hollowed directly into the floating rock.

As their shadows crossed the outer terrace, four figures dropped from the upper roosts like stooping falcons.

They were Aarakocra sentinels clad in polished silver mail and cerulean tabards, gripping slender sky-glaives and silver-tipped javelins. They formed an impenetrable perimeter, their talons clicking against the polished white marble.

Tvyordiy did not draw a weapon. He landed with a low, respectful sweep of his wings, bowing from the waist with the quiet dignity of a monastic pilgrim. Reaching beneath his traveling robes, he drew forth the ancient silver disc—the Tessera of Harek—and raised it flat upon his open palm.

The lead sentinel stepped forward, his eagle-like visage lined with years of border skirmishes. He was broad-shouldered, feathered in rich chestnut and cream, bearing a heavy sky-glaive notched from dozens of aerial duels: **Wing-Captain Deekek**.

Deekek's golden eyes dropped to the coin. He stiffened.

> *"The Tessera of the Western Gale,"* Deekek breathed, his voice a rasp of stunned disbelief. He looked up, his gaze raking across Tvyordiy’s face—the distinctive sunburst orange plumage encircling his eyes, the heavy, unbending arch of his brow, the broad, powerful shoulders that mirrored the line of the aerie's greatest vanguard sentinels. *"By the Four Winds... Harek’s coin."*

Deekek took a step forward, his hand trembling as he reached out to touch the silver edge.

> *"Twenty summers,"* the captain murmured, his Auran dialect thick with ancestral music. *"I was three winters fledged when the storm cult broke through the lower perimeter in 1480 DR. I saw Harek and Vaelen plant their talons into the stone of the Lower Perch. They told us to fly the crèche upward and never look back. When the smoke cleared, the perch was shattered, and their bodies were gone. For twenty summers, brother... I believed the lower world had swallowed our bloodline whole."*

Deekek wrapped his great wings around Tvyordiy in a fierce, concussive warrior's embrace. For the first time since parting with Myagkiy on the battlements of Aufox Manor, Tvyordiy's composure softened, his throat tightening with deep, wordless gratitude.

---

### 🪽 The Clutch-Twin: Klee'errk (Aial Sun-Catcher)

A sudden, piercing cry cut through the upper tiers.

Descending from the high watchtower like a falling sunbeam came another sentinel. She touched down upon the marble without a sound, her silver aerial armor polished to a mirror sheen, her long-hafted feather-spear gripped in an effortless warrior's balance.

Tvyordiy froze.

She was twenty summers old. Her plumage was an incandescent, sunburst blaze of orange and pristine snow-white—an exact, living mirror of his own feathers. Her golden eyes were fierce, clear, and razor-sharp, burning with an unblemished grace that spoke of a lifetime spent riding the untainted gales of the Upper Heavens. In her grip was their mother Vaelen's ceremonial feather-spear of white ash and silver wire.

This was **[[Klee'errk]]**, known among the sky-warriors as **Aial Sun-Catcher**—Sentinel of the High Roost, and Tvyordiy's **surviving clutch-twin sister**.

In Aarakocra biology, females lay only a single egg each year; twin clutches were a rare, sacred miracle occurring once in generations. In the spring of 1480 DR, Vaelen had laid two eggs. When the Talosite raid besieged the Lower Perch, one egg was too fragile to move—its shell bore a hairline fracture from the concussive thunder-stones, unable to survive the violent planar rift. That egg was rushed to the warm, sheltered nurseries of the High Citadel. The other egg, robust and unbroken, was wrapped in Harek's battle-linen, entrusted with the silver Tessera to a courier, and smuggled through an emergency rift to Toril.

Klee'errk walked slowly toward Tvyordiy. She tilted her head, observing the heavy calluses on his talons, the notch in his beak, the battle-scars across his chest, and the coarse, muscular bulk of a martial artist forged in the lowlands.

> *"We were cradled in the same nest,"* Klee'errk whispered, her voice high, musical, and clear as mountain ice. *"Yet you smell of salt, pine-pitch, and cold granite. You carry the weight of an entire world upon your shoulders, brother."*

Tvyordiy bowed his head, spreading his scarred, twelve-foot wings:
> *"I had no sky, sister. So I had to learn how to stand upon the earth without falling."*

Klee'errk lowered her spear. She stepped close, resting her forehead against his shoulder-feathers, sealing the seventeen-year void with silent tears.

---

### 📜 The Sky-Weaver's Revelation: Clan Matron Soolin & The True Name

Led by Deekek and Klee'errk, Tvyordiy and Yeri were escorted into the **Hall of the Cloud-Weft**, the high sanctuary carved into the pinnacle of the spire. Great tapestries of spun mist and silver thread billowed in the cross-breeze, depicting the history of the Aarakocra since the dawn of the elemental realms.

At the center of the hall, seated upon a throne of woven cedar, sat **Clan Matron Soolin**.

She was ancient—well over a hundred summers old, a venerable matriarch whose life spanned generations of sky-weavers. Her crest feathers were wispy and silver as cirrus clouds, and her clouded, milky eyes were blind. Yet as Tvyordiy crossed the threshold, she turned her head directly toward him, her nostrils flaring as she caught his scent.

"Bring him forward," the Matron rasped, her voice like dry autumn leaves rustling before a gale. "Bring Vaelen's lost boy to me."

Tvyordiy sank to his knees before the elder. Gently, Soolin raised her withered, featherless talons, tracing the heavy ridges of his brow, the thick muscle of his neck, and the silver frost touching the edges of his crest. Her claws closed around the ancestral Tessera.

> *"I stood wing-to-wing with your mother Vaelen when the thunder-beasts crashed upon our walls in 1480 DR,"* Soolin murmured softly. *"When the Lower Perch burned, your fragile twin sister stayed in the inner aerie, while you were sent into the dark below with your father's seal. Vaelen whispered your name to me as the smoke rose, binding me by the winds to keep it safe until the courier returned. The courier fell; the world below swallowed you. But the wind does not forget."*

Soolin pressed her dry claw against Tvyordiy's breast, right over his pounding heart. She drew in a deep breath of the sacred air, and with the sharp, clicking music of High Auran, she spoke the name aloud:

> # *"Hahrk'errk."*
> *(Pronounced [hɑːrk.ʔɛrk] — High Auran: "The Unyielding Sky" / "The Stone-Gale")*

The word struck the chamber like the crack of an iron quarterstaff.

Between the first syllable and the second was a sharp, unbending glottal stop—a hard, percussive beak-click that acoustically embodied absolute resistance, an acoustic barrier that allowed no softening.

Tvyordiy stood frozen.

In that singular moment, twelve years of memory collapsed into blinding clarity. He saw Myagkiy kneeling in the muck of Fishgut Alley in 1488 DR, extending a warm loaf of barley bread. He remembered Myagkiy tracing the tattered cloth and speaking the name that had defined his existence: **Tvyordiy**—the Hard Sign (**Ъ**).

Myagkiy had never known Auran. He had never heard of the Boundless Whorl. Yet through the mystical resonance of the Signs, Myagkiy's holy spirit had intuitively perceived the unbending, rock-hard cadence of the boy's soul.

He was **Hahrk'errk** by celestial birthright; he was **Tvyordiy Znak** by chosen brotherhood. Both names were the exact same truth: *The Unbending.*

Matron Soolin laughed—a rich, melodic trill that echoed through the high vaults.

> *"The lowlander did not invent your name, child,"* she spoke with quiet wonder. *"His spirit merely heard the wind. The Hard Sign was the mortal echo of your true song. You were never lost, Hahrk'errk. You were only walking the long road home."*

---

### 🍵 A Kenku in the Cloud-Roost: Yeri & The Fledglings

While the elders spoke of bloodlines and destiny, the arrival of [[Yeri]] had sparked an entirely different sensation in the lower courts.

A Kenku on the Elemental Plane of Air was a contradiction in terms—a creature bound by ancient decree to walk the muck. But a Kenku who hovered upon four translucent, prismatic **Dragonfly Wings**, radiating starlight and divine sorcery? To the fledglings of the aerie, she was nothing short of a walking miracle.

Clustered upon the outer balustrade, a dozen young Aarakocra—downy-feathered chicks and wide-eyed fledglings—watched her with open beaks.

Yeri sat cross-legged on a floating cedar bench, her four gossamer wings humming in idle contentment. Seeing her audience, her beak curved into a sharp, deadpan grin. 

"What are you gawking at, fluff-balls?" she muttered in the haughty, crisp accent of a Silverymoon archivist. *"Never seen an archmage with proper aerial appendages?"*

The fledglings chirped with delight, hopping closer across the polished floor.

With a flick of her claw, Yeri conjured a bobbing globe of dancing violet starlight via *Prestidigitation*. The chicks squealed, chasing the orb across the court like kittens. Pulling a small brass kettle from her traveling pack, Yeri filled it with mountain runoff, snapped her fingers to kindle a miniature *Fire Bolt* beneath the spout with pinpoint restraint, and began steeping dried mountain pine-needles and wild jasmine.

As the fragrant steam rose, she began to whistle.

It began as a mimicry of the wind-flutes carved into the cliffs, but soon shifted—weaving in the tavern ballads of Neverwinter, the crying gulls of the Sword Coast docks, and the rhythmic percussion of dwarven mining songs. The fledglings sat in an enraptured circle around her, whistling back in imperfect, joyful harmonies, sharing dried cloud-dates and sipping tea from her tin cups.

Watching from the colonnade, Tvyordiy felt a quiet, profound warmth settle in his chest. Yeri had spent her life an exile, despised as a thief and a wingless beggar. Here, beneath the open sky, she was an honored guest, teaching song to the dawn.

---

### 🌑 The Boar Beneath the Feathers: Inner Conflict on the Western Perch

As the ambient light of the plane softened into the luminous sapphire twilight of evening, Tvyordiy walked alone to the **Western Perch**.

It was the highest forward observation ledge of the citadel—the very platform where his father Harek and mother Vaelen had stood sentinel thirty years ago. Below, the cloud-reefs drifted like islands in a sea of liquid lapis lazuli. The air was sweet, clean, and untainted by mortal rot.

Yet as he gripped the edge of the stone with his talons, a heavy, sickening throb pulsed in the center of his chest.

The beast was awake.

The boar-horns of the Talosite vanguard still echoed in his skull. Beneath his feathers, his skin burned with an unnatural, feverish heat. He looked down at his own talons—the hands that had broken jaws in Waterdeep’s alleys, that had torn through troll flesh on the Evermoor, that carried the dark curse of the wereboar sleeping in his blood.

*How can I stand here?* the agonizing thought tore through him. *This is the sacred sky. Untainted. Innocent. And I am a creature of the mud. A beast of swine-tusks and slaughter, wrapped in feathers.*

He recalled how [[Myagkiy Znak]] had offered his healing hands to cleanse the lycanthropy after the battles of Leilon. Tvyordiy had refused. He had told himself it was to understand his enemies, to bear the burden of the curse so others would not. But standing in his ancestral home, the truth felt ugly and corrupt: he carried the filth of the lower world into heaven.

"The wind does not apologize for carrying the mountain's dust," a voice spoke from the shadows.

Tvyordiy turned. Klee'errk stood in the archway, her mother's feather-spear resting against her shoulder. Her golden eyes were calm, but they saw through him with piercing clarity.

"I saw how you breathed when that boar-horn sounded across the cataract," she said quietly, stepping onto the ledge beside him. "There is a beast sleeping inside your ribs, brother. A heavy, rooting creature of the mud."

Tvyordiy bowed his head, shame tightening his throat. "It is a curse. A foul taint I contracted in the world below. I have kept it chained with monastic *ki*... but I fear it has stained my soul. This sky... it is too pure for what I have become."

Klee'errk walked to the very edge of the abyss, looking north toward the gathering, necrotic purple thunderheads of Talos.

"Do you know why our parents died on the Lower Perch twenty years ago, Hahrk'errk?" she asked, her voice hard and ringing. "Our warriors fought with grace. We danced upon the thermals. We hurled silver javelins and sang sacred hymns to the wind. But when the Destroyer’s horde broke through, they did not bring grace. They brought thunder-iron, siege-hammers, and the brute savagery of mad swine. Grace alone cannot stop an avalanche."

She turned, placing a firm, feathered hand upon his heavily callused fist.

"The priests say the sky is pure," Klee'errk continued, her eyes blazing with fierce resolve. "But the storm is brutal. The tempest of Talos will not be broken by elegant feathers. It must be broken by an unyielding wall. If you carry the beast of the mud within you, do not weep for it. *Master it.* Let the beast be your iron hide, and let your monastic spirit be the spear. We will need both before the next sun rises."

Tvyordiy stared at his twin sister. The suffocating weight upon his chest lifted like fog before a gale.

He remembered the words of Elder Kaya at the Grandfather Tree: *The beast is not an enemy to be starved, but a brother to be given a seat at the hearth.*

Tvyordiy drew in a deep, diaphragmatic breath of the boundless sky. The boar within did not disappear, but its thrashing ceased. It settled into the dark warmth of his bones, rooted, quiet, and obedient—ready to lend its unstoppable brawn to the defense of the aerie.

He nodded to his sister, his subterranean baritone rumbling with newfound harmony:
> *"When the storm comes, the willow bends, but the stone remains. We are the stone."*
