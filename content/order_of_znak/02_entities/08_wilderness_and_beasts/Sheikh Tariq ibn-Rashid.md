# 🏜️ Sheikh Tariq ibn-Rashid

## 🛡️ Overview & Character Lore
* **Identity & Heritage:** Chieftain, war-leader, and respected patriarch of **Clan Bait Mahra**, one of the proud Bedine nomad tribes wandering the southern wastes of the **Anauroch Desert** (formerly ancient Anauria).
* **Appearance & Demeanor:** Weathered, bronze-skinned warrior in his late 50s with hawk-like dark eyes, a silver-streaked dark beard, and desert-hardened poise. Clad in indigo and sand-colored flowing robes with a silver-chased scimitar at his sash, riding a magnificent dapple-grey war-camel.
* **First Meeting with the Soft Sign (1501 DR):** Encountered [[Myagkiy Znak]] at the landmark **Pillar of Kozah**. Recognizing in Myagkiy's peaceful demeanor and holy sunburst medallion a divine messenger rather than a tomb-crawler or Phaerimm warlock, Sheikh Tariq welcomed the Black battle priest into his great black goat-hair pavilion (*khayma*).
* **Unrolling the Gazelle-Hide Map:** Beside the campfire, Sheikh Tariq shared his clan's most guarded heirloom: an ancient gazelle-hide map revealing the location of the sunken black glass pyramid of **Aul-Khem**. Distraught by the abduction of his nephews and tribal youth by Netherese death cultists, he accepted Myagkiy's pledge to enter the tomb unarmed and deliver the captives.
* **Providing the Scouts:** Assigned his swiftest and bravest camel scouts—[[Kahlil and Zayd]]—to guide Myagkiy across the salt flats and dunes to Aul-Khem.
* **The Desert Crusade & Tribal Adoption (1502–1505 DR):** After Myagkiy miraculously revived the three suffocated herders and returned them to the clan, Sheikh Tariq proclaimed Myagkiy *Al-Khatib al-Latif* (the Gentle Preacher). During the winter desert campaign, Tariq coordinated nomad outriders to assist Myagkiy in purging Myrkulite cells across the dunes. On the eve of Myagkiy's return to the west, Sheikh Tariq and tribal shamans presided over a sacred council ceremony, formally adopting Myagkiy into Clan Bait Mahra and inscribing thematic geometric tribal tattoos upon his temples.
